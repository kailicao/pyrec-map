Directions from MESA

Part of A new design for YREC. The directions in which pyrec could grow beyond YREC, family by family, from a comparison with MESA (Modules for Experiments in Stellar Astrophysics): for each, what MESA does, what YREC does, and where pyrec's interfaces leave room. None is built: The defaults reproduce YREC, and these directions may be implemented later, by pyrec's developers or by its readers. No interface may rule one out (The families).

Sources. MESA: its documentation for release r26.4.1 (controls, changelog, test suite, auto_diff, troubleshooting) and for latest (EOS overview, changelog), and its instrument papers, cited as MESA I to VI (references at the end). YREC: the release paper, Pinsonneault et al. 2026 (arXiv:2603.25792), cited by section; its documentation at commit 7be4133 (physics namelist, control namelist, microphysics) and its KNOWN_ISSUES.md; this site's pages for the code. A MESA feature in main is unreleased as of Nov 18, 2026.

The equation of state (eos)

  • MESA blends its components in order of precedence, CMS (experimental, off by default), Skye, PC, FreeEOS, OPAL/SCVH, and HELM, with an ideal-gas backstop (EOS overview); MESA VI describes the default blend with Skye (its §4), which is on by default since r21.12.1.
  • YREC recommends the OPAL 2005/2006 EOS (LOPALE06), uses SCV (Saumon et al. 1995) outside OPAL's table (LSCV), and keeps the Yale EOS (Saha, degeneracy from Fermi tables, Debye-Hückel) as a backup, whose semi-analytic derivatives serve the solution. The tie-breaker is OPAL > SCV > Yale, ramped near the tables' edges, where thermodynamic consistency is not preserved; OPAL's metal content is fixed per run, so settling cannot change it (paper §2.3.1; microphysics; physics namelist).
  • Room in pyrec. The EOS's point takes the point's full composition, not only X and Z with one metal mixture per run, and returns every derivative the solver uses: A family blending several EOSs consistently over any composition fits the interface.

Opacities (opacity)

  • MESA's kap module gives radiative and conductive opacities (home page); OPLIB Type 1 tables were added in r24.08.1 (changelog), and MESA VI updates the molecular, Compton, and conductive opacities.
  • YREC has conductive opacities (Cassisi et al. 2007 in the paper; Potekhin's tables and routine in the code, condopacp.f), molecular opacities (Ferguson et al. 2005, YREC's ALEX06 tables, below $\log T = 4.0$), atomic ones (OP or OPAL, above 4.1, with a ramp between), and OPAL's C/O-rich tables for helium-burning regions, each table for a fixed heavy-element mixture (paper §2.3.2; getopac.f).
  • Room in pyrec. The opacity's point takes X, Z, and the ionization, and its tables are the family's data: Another table set is another family.

Atmospheres, the boundary, and starspots (atmosphere)

  • MESA offers atm_option T_tau, table, irradiated_grey, fixed_Teff, fixed_Tsurf, fixed_Psurf, and fixed_Psurf_and_Tsurf. Its $T$–$\tau$ relations are Eddington, solar Hopf, Krishna Swamy, and Trampedach solar, and its tables photosphere, tau_100 (the default), tau_10, tau_1, tau_1m1, WD_tau_25, and DB_WD_tau_25 (controls). Starspots came in MESA VI, which implements YREC's SPOTS treatment and calls it "equivalent": the same filling factor and contrast, the boundary condition modified through a magnetic pressure, and the radiative gradient in the convection zone reduced, so that MESA's spots act in the interior too (MESA VI, §7.1).
  • YREC has KTTAU 0 (Eddington gray), 1 (Krishna Swamy), 2 (the Harvard-Smithsonian reference atmosphere), 3 (Kurucz 1993), 4 (Allard and Hauschildt 1995; solar Z only), and 5 (Castelli and Kurucz 2003), the tables falling back to gray off their hot edge (envint.f, lines 228–266); its outer fitting point lies well below the photosphere, with the envelope integrated at three ($L$, $T_\mathrm{eff}$) points, the "envelope triangle" (paper §2.2, §2.3.6; surfbc.f). Its starspots block flux and modify the surface boundary condition, with the filling factor and contrast fixed for a run (SPOTF, SPOTX; paper §2.5).
  • Room in pyrec. What the solver needs from an atmosphere family is the fitting point's $\log P$, $\log T$, and $\log r$ as functions of $L$ and $T_\mathrm{eff}$, with their derivatives; the $T$–$\tau$ relation, the tables, the fitting mass, and the triangle are the default's own. A photosphere condition, an irradiated surface, or another table set is another family.

Nuclear reactions and neutrinos (network)

  • MESA has fully coupled networks "with hundreds of isotopes" (MESA III), weak rates on the fly (MESA III), operator-split burning (MESA VI), an other_screening hook (r21.12.1), and thermal neutrino losses in its neu module (home page).
  • YREC has a fixed network: non-equilibrium burning of H, ³He, ⁴He, ¹²C, ¹³C, ¹⁴N, ¹⁶O, and ¹⁸O (the pp chain, CNO, triple alpha, and three alpha captures), pre-main-sequence deuterium burning, and ⁶Li, ⁷Li, and ⁹Be as trace species; weak screening by default, intermediate and strong available; thermal neutrino losses from Itoh et al. 1996, and solar neutrino fluxes (paper §2.3.4; engeb.f; sneut.f). It is not adaptive, and its high-mass runs end at core helium exhaustion.
  • Room in pyrec. The network's size and species are the family's data (species, rate_names), and its point reads the composition array whole; a larger network, other rates, or other screening is a subclass with its own kernel.

Convection and mixing (convection)

  • MESA offers MLT_option Cox, ML1, ML2, Mihalas, Henyey, TDC (time-dependent convection, the default in r26.4.1, introduced in MESA VI), and none, with the Ledoux criterion optional; overshoot schemes exponential, step, and other; semiconvection (Langer 1985) and thermohaline mixing (Kippenhahn; Traxler et al. 2011; Brown et al. 2013), both with the Ledoux criterion (controls; MESA II, IV, V, VI).
  • YREC has Böhm-Vitense's MLT only, with the Schwarzschild criterion, and an option to take the adiabatic gradient in convective cores; step overshoot in pressure scale heights for cores, envelopes, and intermediate zones, as overmixing, with adiabatic overshoot as an experimental option. It has semiconvection after Castellani et al. 1971 (LSEMIC). Thermohaline mixing, MLT variants, exponential overshoot, and time-dependent convection are not in YREC (paper §2.3.3; tpgrad.f; oversh.f; sconvec.f).
  • Room in pyrec. The variant of the gradient is the class's, not the signature's; mixing enters as diffusion coefficients (mixing_coefficients) that overshoot, semiconvection, thermohaline mixing, and rotation can each add to. The gradient's point does not yet take a composition gradient (for the Ledoux criterion and thermohaline mixing) or the previous velocity and step (for time-dependent convection): an input each, to be added when such a family is written.

Diffusion and settling (diffusion)

  • MESA has element diffusion (do_element_diffusion; MESA IV, coefficients in VI) and radiative levitation since MESA III, improved in V and VI (controls).
  • YREC has settling and thermal diffusion (Burgers' equations as solved by Thoul et al. 1994) for helium, the metals as one group, and ⁶Li, ⁷Li, and ⁹Be (LDIFY, LDIFZ, LDIFLI); the paper names radiative levitation but says YREC includes settling and thermal diffusion only (paper §2.4; microdiff.f).
  • Room in pyrec. The diffusion stage takes the State whole and extra diffusion coefficients from other mixing, and gives its own (coefficients), so that a family with more species or radiative accelerations fits.

Rotation (rotation)

  • MESA treats shellular rotation; since MESA V its distortion factors are analytic fits to the Roche potential of a point mass, valid to about 0.9 of critical rotation. It transports angular momentum and composition by diffusion (MESA II), with a Spruit-Tayler dynamo term (D_ST; controls); angular-momentum-conserving remapping is in main (changelog, latest).
  • YREC treats rotation in pseudo-2D: uniform rotation on equipotentials, whose shapes it solves with the potential's quadrupole term, and correction factors in the structure equations (paper §2.6.2; fpft.f); its transport covers meridional circulation, secular shear, and the GSF instability, with μ-gradient inhibition and a free mixing efficiency, and magnetic transport two ways, forced solid-body rotation and a constant extra diffusion (paper §2.6.3, §2.6.5; vcirc.f). MESA's line of treatment descends from YREC's (Heger et al. 2000, from Pinsonneault et al. 1989).
  • Angular-momentum loss. In MESA, a magnetic wind's torque is written by the user, through the other_torque hook; its test case magnetic_braking brakes a 15 M☉ star this way, after Weber and Davis 1967 (magnetic_braking), and accreted_material_j adds the angular momentum that accreted mass carries (accreted_material_j). YREC has three wind laws for low-mass stars, with saturation and Rossby scaling (Krishnamurthi et al. 1997; van Saders and Pinsonneault 2013, after Matt et al. 2012; a custom law), and disk locking for a set time at a set period (paper §2.6.4; nml2).
  • Room in pyrec. Transport, the surface's angular-momentum loss (torque), and the mixing (mixing_coefficients) are separate methods of the base class, so a family can change one alone.

Mass loss and accretion (mass_loss)

  • MESA offers the wind schemes Reimers, Blocker, de Jager, van Loon, Nieuwenhuijzen, Vink, Dutch, and Bjorklund (for hot winds, the red giant branch, and the AGB; controls); its accretion treatment improved in MESA III.
  • YREC's mass loss is experimental and partly implemented: LMDOT with a constant rate (loss or gain) or a Reimers law (physics namelist; massloss.f; KNOWN_ISSUES.md, issue 12).
  • Room in pyrec. The base class has the signed rate (rate: negative for winds, positive for accretion), which the stage applies; the recipe is the family's.

The solver: derivatives and diagnostics (solver)

  • MESA solves structure and composition fully coupled (MESA I), with derivatives carried by its auto_diff types, generated in forward mode; assigning a derivative-carrying value to a plain real is not supported, "because we don't want to accidentally lose the derivative information" (auto_diff). Residual plots are in main (changelog, latest). Energy conservation is central to MESA V and VI.
  • YREC relaxes the four structure equations by Henyey's method, with the composition advanced apart, in up to four iteration levels; its derivatives are analytic, with numerical ones as a slow option; its notes record that the Jacobian and the EOS's second derivatives are not consistent (KNOWN_ISSUES.md, issue 40) and a period-2 limit cycle in the corrections (issue 41) (paper §2.2, §2.3.5; crrect.f). Its .short file logs each iteration's convergence; no global energy-conservation diagnostic was found.
  • Room in pyrec. The solver takes its derivatives from the physics families' outputs, never by differentiating a family, so a family may supply them by automatic differentiation; residuals is declared for diagnostics, and a failed solve is returned, not raised.

The time step and failed steps (timestep)

  • MESA has "sophisticated timestep controls" (MESA I; time_delta_coeff, varcontrol_target), with soft and hard limits on changes, and retries and redos (max_number_retries; controls).
  • YREC takes the most restrictive of several limits (burning, structure changes on the pre-main sequence, rotation, the growth per step; paper §2.2; htimer.f). It does not retry: A model that fails to converge stops the run (starin.f, lines 227–235), and KNOWN_ISSUES.md notes that "A step retry at a smaller timestep after a failed solve would add robustness" (issue 40).
  • Room in pyrec. The model may retry a failed step from the start of the step with a shorter step; the default stops, as YREC does.

The mesh (rezoner)

  • MESA has an adaptive mesh (MESA I; mesh_delta_coeff, max_allowed_nz; controls).
  • YREC rezones at the start of each step by limits on $\Delta\log m$, $\Delta L$, and $\Delta\log P$ in the envelope, at the base of the convection zone, and in the core, keeping the discontinuities in X, Z, and $\Omega$ (paper §2.2; hpoint.f).
  • Room in pyrec. The rezoner's controls are its own configuration, and the shells' number is the State's.

Numerics beyond the families

YREC is fast: On one thread its release paper measures it at about 8, 2, and 6 times faster than MESA r24.08.1 from the pre-main sequence to the ZAMS, from the ZAMS to the end of the main sequence, and from there to the tip of the red-giant branch, at similar mesh (paper §3.1.1). Many of MESA's numerical choices buy robustness and stability at some cost in speed, so in pyrec each is weighed against what it costs a run.

  • Energy accounting. Energy conservation, including during changes of mass, is central to MESA V and VI (V; VI). No global energy-conservation diagnostic was found in YREC. In pyrec it would be a diagnostic computed from the State after each step, an output beside the history.
  • Failed steps. MESA removed its backups in r15140 ("MESA no longer has the concept of a 'backup'"; changelog) and keeps retries and redos (controls). YREC stops the run when a model fails to converge (The time step and failed steps).
  • Parallelism. MESA runs one model on several threads with OpenMP (MESA I; its performance measured in MESA V), and its testing runs many cases side by side (Wolf et al. 2023). The Fortran of YREC is single-threaded, and its Python package runs many models in parallel (python_package.md). pyrec keeps each model on one thread and runs many models side by side, so that the core-hours of a grid are saved; threads inside a model are a technique to try and report, not the default.
  • Reproducibility. MESA III claimed "bit-for-bit consistency for all results across all the supported platforms" (III); the testing paper finds most of MESA written to be bit-for-bit consistent across platforms, with checksums not yet tracked for some cases (Wolf et al. 2023), and the MESA SDK gives a unified build (MESA II). YREC's tests compare outputs with references within a tolerance, and the references are not under version control (KNOWN_ISSUES.md, issue 32). pyrec's outputs are compared with YREC's byte for byte, and a copy of a model run on must match the original byte for byte (The State).

Beyond the families

  • User hooks. MESA has run_star_extras with use_other_* hooks (controls); YREC "was not designed with modular user inputs in mind" (paper §1), and users edit the source. In pyrec, the step's stages, the hooks between them, the controls at the start of each step, and the retry are where a reader adds new physics without editing the framework (the overview, Looking inside).
  • Oscillation codes and photometry. MESA links GYRE and ADIPLS, supporting the interfaces, not the packages (troubleshooting; MESA II, III), and has synthetic photometry (colors) since r25.12.1 (changelog). YREC writes its own pulsation files along the track (pdist.f), with no GYRE or ADIPLS binding. In pyrec these would be outputs of the model, beside the history.
  • Binaries, hydrodynamics, and radial pulsation. MESA has a binary module and implicit hydrodynamics with shocks (MESA III), a Riemann solver and STELLA coupling (MESA IV), and radial stellar pulsation, RSP (MESA V); none is in YREC, which is hydrostatic.
  • Evolutionary phases. MESA evolves stars through the helium flash (MESA V), the AGB's thermal pulses (MESA I), to cooling white dwarfs (MESA IV), and to core collapse (MESA I, III). YREC ends a run at helium ignition in a degenerate core, making low-mass core-helium-burning models by rescaling instead (KNOWN_ISSUES.md, issue 33); the AGB is outside the domain it is optimized for, no white-dwarf case was found, and its high-mass runs end at core helium exhaustion (paper §1.1, §3.3.2).
