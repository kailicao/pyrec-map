Explanation

Purpose. This file computes the nuclear energy generation rate at one point, its derivatives with respect to density and temperature, the rates of the individual reactions (for the abundance changes), the solar neutrino fluxes and, in hot cores, the neutrino energy losses. Its long header, mostly by John Bahcall (JNB, 1990–1997) and Marc Pinsonneault (MHP), documents the reaction data: rates from Neutrino Astrophysics (Bahcall 1989) and the Solar Fusion Workshop (Adelberger et al. 1998), energy releases from Bahcall and Ulrich (1988, RMP 60, 297), screening from Salpeter (1954) and Graboske et al. (1973), alpha captures from Caughlan and Fowler (1988), neutrino losses from Itoh et al. (1996) or an older fit.

How it fits.

  • Inputs: the arguments DL, TL ($\log\rho$, $\log T$), the mass fractions X, Y, XHE3, XC12, XC13, XN14, XO16, XO18, XH2, and IU (the point's index, used to store the rates and for the deuterium cap). From the modules: the derivative factors QS0E, QQS0EE and the N15+p branch factors FO16, FC12 (CROSS_MOD, set by REMAP: all 1, or with LNEWNUC from the namelist's cross-sections and their derivatives); from NMLPAR_MOD, the cross-section factors SSTANDARD(17), WEAKSCREENING (0.03 by default), the cut-off temperatures TCUT, LSNU (compute neutrino fluxes) and LNULOS1 (Itoh losses); JCZ (DEUTER_MOD) and TAUCZ (OVRTRN_MOD) for the deuterium cap; CLN, CC13, CC23 and CAVOG (Avogadro's number, $1/m_u$, for the deuterium rate) from CONSTANTS_MOD.
  • Who calls it: COEFFT (every point, every iteration), the main program (main.f, for the neutrino fluxes and Be-7 of the output) and YTIME (the time step).
  • What it calls: NEUTRINO (the Itoh et al. losses), when LNULOS1.
  • Outputs:
  • arguments: EPP1, EPP2, EPP3 (energy from the pp chain's branches), ECN (CNO), E3AL (triple alpha), SUM1 (the total, erg g$^{-1}$ s$^{-1}$), PEP, PET (its derivatives), and per point HR1…HR13 (the reaction rates, per Gyr per amu) and HF1 = $f_3$, HF2 = $f_1$ (branching fractions); the abundance routines (KEMCOM and others) use the rates, per the comment; EALPCA (energy from alpha captures) and ENU (neutrino losses), read by COEFFT and YTIME right after their calls (main.f passes locals it does not use); BE7MASSFRACTION, set only with LSNU and read only by main.f, which keeps it between calls by its SAVE;
  • modules: FLUX(10) (FLUXES_MOD, neutrino fluxes at Earth per gram, read by COEFFT, main.f, WRTOUT); ENGHE3, HE3ALL (GRAB_MOD).

The method.

  1. Screening. With the mass fractions over 13 isotopes, $\mu_I^{-1} = \sum X_i/A_i$, $\mu_e^{-1} = \sum Z_iX_i/A_i$, $\zeta = \sum Z_i^2X_i/A_i$, and a degeneracy factor $f'/f$ (a fit to Salpeter's figure 1 in $\log E_F/kT$): $$\Lambda_0 = 5.9426\times10^{-6}\,T_9^{-3/2}\sqrt{\rho/\mu_I}, \qquad U_\mathrm{weak} = Z_1Z_2\,\Lambda_0\,\sqrt{\frac{\zeta + (f'/f)\,\mu_e^{-1}}{\mu_I^{-1}}}.$$ Weak screening (Salpeter 1954) if $U_\mathrm{weak} \le$ WEAKSCREENING; otherwise Graboske et al.'s intermediate form ($U \le 2$), or the strong form ($U \ge 5$ or where it is smaller). The rate is multiplied by $e^{U}$, with $\partial U/\partial\ln\rho$ and $\partial U/\partial\ln T$.

  2. pp chain and CNO (reactions 1–7: pp, $^3$He+$^3$He, $^3$He+$^4$He, p+$^{12}$C, p+$^{13}$C, p+$^{14}$N, p+$^{16}$O), in the form of Neutrino Astrophysics eq. 3.14 and Fowler, Caughlan and Zimmerman: $$r_j = \rho\left[T_9^{-2/3} + Q_1T_9^{-1/3} + s'_j(Q_2 + Q_3T_9^{1/3}) + s''_j(Q_4T_9^{2/3} + Q_5T_9)\right]\exp\!\left(Q_6T_9^{-1/3} + Q_7 + (Q_8T_9)^2 + U_j\right)S_j,$$ where $T_9 = T/10^9$ K; $Q_6T_9^{-1/3}$ is $-\tau$, the comments' TAU of Neutrino Astrophysics eq. 3.10 ("NOTE Q6 IS NEGATIVE"), $Q_7$ "the constant in front of the reaction rate", a logarithm that carries $-\ln S_0$ ($S_0$ in keV b), $-\ln(A_0A_1)$ and $-\ln(1+\delta_{01})$ by the comments' formula, $Q_1$–$Q_5$ the terms of the expansion of $S(E)$ about the Gamow peak (from $S'$ and $S''$), and $Q_8$ a high-temperature cutoff (zero for these reactions); $S_j$ = SSTANDARD(J) is a dimensionless factor on the reference cross section (1 for the coded value; the comments list the sets that reproduce published solar models, such as BP03 or the Gran Sasso 2009 values); $s'$, $s''$ = QS0E, QQS0EE (user factors on the S-factor derivatives, MHP 8/14), and the logarithmic derivatives DRATRO, DRATT (the temperature derivative of the bracket carries the same factors $s'_j$, $s''_j$).

  3. Branchings. $^7$Be: electron capture against proton capture (with its own screening), $f_1$, $f_2$; $^{15}$N+p: ($p,\alpha$) against ($p,\gamma$), $f_3$, $f_4$.

  4. Alpha captures (above TCUT(4)): $^{13}$C($\alpha$,n), $^{12}$C($\alpha,\gamma$), $^{14}$N($\alpha,\gamma$), and triple alpha, $r_{3\alpha} \propto \rho^2T_9^{-3}e^{-4.4027/T_9}$.
  5. Deuterium burning (if $X_D > 10^{-11}$, and like every rate here only above TCUT(1): below it the routine zeroes all rates, the deuterium rate included, before reaching this block; the documentation calls the default TCUT(1) = 6.5 "bad for D burning in preMS", and every test-suite run sets TCUT(1) = 5.5), as in DEUTRATE (the rate $\propto T_9^{-2/3}$, times $N_A/A_D$ = CAVOG/ANUC(3)), with $Q = 5.494$ MeV; its logarithmic temperature derivative QRTDEUT is that of the same form. In the convective envelope (IU $\ge$ JCZ), when TAUCZ $> 1$, the rate is capped so that $r X \le (N_A/A_D)/\tau_\mathrm{cz}$ (RDEUTMAX).
  6. Energy. $\epsilon_j = r_j X_aX_b\,Q_j$, with the $Q$ values in MeV net of neutrino losses: for example 6.664 for pp, 12.860 for $^3$He+$^3$He, and $1.586 + 17.394f_1 + 11.499f_2$ for $^3$He+$^4$He. A comment beside the pp value records a 1997 lowering by 0.002 MeV for a better estimate of the neutrino energy loss; the code does not say which reactions the 6.664 MeV covers (question 11). Then SUM1 $= \sum\epsilon_j$, PEP $= \sum\epsilon_j\,\partial\ln r_j/\partial\ln\rho$, PET likewise in $T$: absolute derivatives, $\partial\epsilon/\partial\ln\rho$ and $\partial\epsilon/\partial\ln T$, which is how COEFFT uses them.
  7. Rates for the abundances: HR$_j$ = $r_j C_{21}$, with $C_{21}$ = (s per $10^9$ yr) × (amu in g), per the comment.
  8. Neutrino fluxes (if LSNU and $X > 10^{-6}$): pp, pep, hep, $^7$Be, $^8$B, $^{13}$N, $^{15}$O, $^{17}$F and two "fictional" diagnostics, per gram at 1 AU, in $10^{10}$ cm$^{-2}$ s$^{-1}$.
  9. Neutrino losses (above TCUT(5)): from NEUTRINO (Itoh et al. 1996) or the older fits for pair, photo- and plasma neutrinos; the loss is subtracted from SUM1 and its derivatives from PEP, PET.

Design. About half the file is comments: the history of every constant, and sets of SSTANDARD from published solar models (BP92, BP95, BP03, Gran Sasso 2009, …). Reactions 8–13 are partly "holdovers from the earlier Yale code"; 9 and 13 are zeroed.

Questions for the experts

  1. The Itoh branch's temperature derivative. PET = PEP + DSNUDT should almost certainly be PET = PET + DSNUDT: as written, the temperature derivative of $\epsilon$ is replaced by the density derivative.
  2. Relative and absolute derivatives. In the Itoh branch, DSNUDT and DSNUDD are made relative ($\partial\ln\epsilon_\nu/\partial\ln T$) and then added to PET, PEP, which are absolute ($\partial\epsilon/\partial\ln T$). The older branch adds absolute terms. Should it be ENU*DSNUDT?
  3. Alpha-capture derivatives. For reactions 8, 10 and 11, DRATT = DSCT + RHO/RATE*(…) divides the derivative of the bracket by the whole rate, which includes the constant ($\sim10^{22}$) and $e^U$; the derivative of the bracket alone is then off by that factor, and DRATT is close to the screening term only. Also, in reaction 10, the derivatives of $R_1^2$ and $R_2^2$ lack a factor $T_9^{-2/3}$; in reaction 8, the derivative terms of A2…A5 lack the factor $T_9^{-3/2}$ that multiplies them in the rate.
  4. A comment that contradicts the code. "MHP 5/90 change derivatives to be d ln eps/d ln rho …": the code returns PEP = SUM2, not divided by SUM1, so the derivatives are $\partial\epsilon/\partial\ln\rho$. The comment should be updated.
  5. The low-temperature path. Below TCUT(1) the routine jumps to label 200 without setting SUM1, F1, F3, which keep the previous call's values (SAVE); HF1, HF2 and the returned SUM1 may be stale. On that path RHO, T9 and the $^7$Be rates are stale too, and with LSNU they enter FLUX(3) and BE7MASSFRACTION. COEFFT only calls above TCUT(1), but main.f's call checks only $\log T \ge 6$.
  6. The index passed by main.f (KC's change). main.f's call was changed from passing I to passing IU, which main.f never sets; HR1(IU)…HF2(IU) are then written at an undefined index, and the deuterium cap compares an undefined IU with JCZ. Probably I was meant (see main.f, around line 388).
  7. Hard-coded thresholds. Rates below $10^{-30}$ (before) and $10^{-5}$ (after) are set to zero; SUM1 $\le 10^{-12}$ zeroes PEP, PET and the EG array (so the neutrino fluxes), but keeps SUM1 itself and the DG array (so EPP1, ECN, …). Are these still appropriate, and should SUM1 be zeroed too?
  8. The default TCUT(1). All rates, deuterium burning included, are zero for $\log T \le$ TCUT(1), 6.5 by default, which the documentation calls "bad for D burning in preMS" and the test suite sets 5.5 everywhere. Should the default be lowered, or the deuterium term be taken out of the gate? The same cut stops pp burning and $^3$He production below $\log T = 6.5$, which a reader would expect to matter for the $^3$He profile of the Sun's outer core and for stars near the hydrogen-burning limit (the test suite's 0.1 $M_\odot$ run), whose central temperature is close to it.
  9. The default screening. The code's default WEAKSCREENING = 0.03 uses weak screening only where $U_\mathrm{weak} \le 0.03$, so where $U_\mathrm{weak}$ exceeds 0.03 (in the solar core, a reader would expect) the pp and CNO rates take Graboske et al.'s intermediate form; the comment calls 0.03 the value for the standard solar model. The YREC paper (§2.3.4) says the default settings recommend weak screening for general use, and every test-suite run sets WEAKSCREENING = 30 (weak screening always). Which is meant to be the default, and how much does the choice change the solar calibration?
  10. Neutrino losses only above TCUT(5). Plasma, photo- and pair-neutrino losses are computed only above $\log T$ = 7.5 (default and test suite). Is that low enough for the degenerate helium cores of low-mass red giants, where plasma neutrinos cool the centre and move the helium ignition off centre?
  11. What the pp $Q$ value covers. The value 6.664 MeV matches p+p→d followed at once by d+p→³He, net of the pp neutrino's mean energy: 1.442 − 0.265 + 5.494 ≈ 6.67 MeV (only the 5.494 MeV of d+p is in the code, as deuterium's QDEUT; KEMCOM's $-3r_1X^2$ does take three protons per pp reaction). Is that what 6.664 stands for, and where does the 0.007 MeV difference come from (the comment accounts for 0.002 MeV)? If so, the deuterium made by the pp reaction is taken to burn at once, and step 5 burns only the deuterium already present, so that nothing is counted twice: is that the intent?
