Explanation

Purpose. The entry point of the neutrino-loss routines added by Grant Newsham (9/06). It sets up a four-species composition (hydrogen, helium, ¹²C, ¹⁶O) and returns the neutrino energy-loss rate SNU with its derivatives with respect to temperature and density, DSNUDT and DSNUDD.

How it fits.

  • Inputs: $T$ (K) and $\rho$ (g cm⁻³), unlogged; $X$, $Y$; ZC and ZO, which ENGEB sets to ¹²C + ¹³C and ¹⁶O + ¹⁸O.
  • Who calls it: only ENGEB, when LNULOS1 is set and $\log T$ is above the cutoff TCUT(5). ENGEB uses $-$SNU as the loss and turns the derivatives into logarithmic ones.
  • What it calls: NULOSSES.
  • Outputs: SNU, DSNUDT, DSNUDD. The units are those of SNEUT (probably erg g⁻¹ s⁻¹, as in the Timmes codes it comes from; not stated here).
  • Modules: none.

The method. Nuclei with $(A, Z)$ = (1, 1), (4, 2), (12, 6), (16, 8), mass fractions $(X, Y, X_\mathrm{C}, X_\mathrm{O})$; everything else is done in NULOSSES.

Changed since rp_1. Declarations only: IMPLICIT NONE.

Questions for the experts

  1. Metals other than C and O. Neon, nitrogen, iron and the rest of $Z$ are left out, so the four mass fractions sum to less than one, and AZBAR does not renormalize them: $\bar A = 1/\sum_i X_i/A_i$ is then too large by the factor $1/\sum_i X_i$, and so is $\bar Z$. Should the fractions be renormalized, or the missing metals added (for example as ¹⁶O)?

11def neutrino(temp, den, x, y, zc, zo, snu, dsnudt, dsnudd):
12    """Set up hydrogen, helium, carbon 12 and oxygen 16 and return the
13    neutrino energy-loss rate with its derivatives in temperature and
14    density, from NULOSSES.
15
16    Translates: neutrino.f#NEUTRINO (09c3b9b)
17
18    Arguments: temp (K) and den (g/cm^3), unlogged; the mass fractions x,
19    y, zc, zo; snu, dsnudt and dsnudd are passed in with any value.
20
21    Returns (snu, dsnudt, dsnudd).
22    """
23    xmass = np.zeros(IONMAX + 1)
24    ymass = np.zeros(IONMAX + 1)
25    # Question (see the page): the other metals are left out, and the four
26    # fractions are not renormalized.
27    aion = np.array([0.0, 1.0, 4.0, 12.0, 16.0])
28    zion = np.array([0.0, 1.0, 2.0, 6.0, 8.0])
29    xmass[1] = x
30    xmass[2] = y
31    xmass[3] = zc
32    xmass[4] = zo

34                                   dsnudt, dsnudd)
35    return snu, dsnudt, dsnudd
