interp.f

43 lines · 1 definitions · on GitHub (7be4133)

Python version: interp.py, 94 lines, on the right (switch to Python above the code).

Purpose

INTERP returns the weights of 4-point Lagrange interpolation, and of its first derivative, at a point $x$.

How it fits

Called by EQSCVE and EQSCVG (the SCV equation of state), GETALEX06 and GETOPAL95 (opacity tables; YALO3D is removed since rp_1), and MICRODIFF_MTE and MODEL_TO_EQUAL (regridding for diffusion). No modules, no calls. The version without derivatives is INTRP2; the 3-point one is INTER3.

The header's "VandenBerg opacity tables" is from an older use. The comment calls it "THE INTERPOLATION ROUTINE FOR THE VANDENBERG OPACITY TABLES(CAPPA)" (lines 16–18↗); it now serves the SCV EOS (EQSCVE, EQSCVG), the opacity tables of GETOPAL95 and GETALEX06, and the diffusion grids (MICRODIFF_MTE, MODEL_TO_EQUAL), so the comment could describe a general four-point Lagrange interpolation.

The method

For nodes $a_1,\dots,a_4$,

$$B_i(x) = \prod_{j\ne i}\frac{x-a_j}{a_i-a_j},\qquad C_i(x) = \frac{dB_i}{dx},$$

returned in B and C; then $f(x)\approx\sum_i B_i f(a_i)$ and $f'(x)\approx\sum_i C_i f(a_i)$. Used in a table, the interpolant is piecewise: it is continuous where the four-point stencil shifts from one cell to the next, but its derivative, and so the derivative weights C the equation of state and opacities use, jump there.

Question 1 (lines 37–40↗). Derivative jumps. Piecewise four-point Lagrange interpolation gives derivatives (such as $\partial\ln\kappa/\partial\ln T$) that jump where the stencil shifts. Do these jumps affect the convergence of the Henyey iteration, which uses them, and would a smoother interpolation (such as the splines some other tables use) be preferable?
