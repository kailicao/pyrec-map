Purpose

Homogenizes the composition of every convection zone (mass-weighted mean over the zone), then applies light clean-ups to the composition.

How it fits

  • Inputs: HCOMP, the shell masses HS2, the convection flags LCZ and M; ZENVM from COMP_MOD; LEXCOM from NMLPAR_MOD.

  • Who calls it: the main program in main.f, GETW (twice) and SECULR. From the main program, after the model has converged ("ENSURE CONVECTION ZONES ARE FULLY MIXED"), it mixes the formal convection zones (LC, without overshoot); from GETW and SECULR, the zones with overshoot built by OVROT (LCZM, LCZ).

    Question 1 (line 977↗). Mixing again after convergence, without overshoot. In the corrector iterations MIX (from CRRECT) has already homogenized the zones with overshoot, yet the main program calls MIXCZ once more after convergence, with the formal flags LC. Is the final mix needed because the zones moved during the iteration, and is it intended that overshoot regions are not re-mixed here?

  • Output: HCOMP in place. It also writes LCZ(M+1) = .FALSE. as a sentinel.

  • Modules: COMP_MOD (ZENVM), NMLPAR_MOD (LEXCOM) and DIMENSIONS_MOD (JSON). A convective turnover time calculation is commented out ("it is now passed in in the OVRTRN common block", G. Somers 3/17); the COMMON blocks rp_1 declared for it (/DEUTER/, /SCRTCH/, /CONST1/, /CONST2/, /ROT/, /MDPHY/, /OVRTRN/) are no longer declared.

The method

  1. Zones. Runs of convective shells give up to 12 zones (MXZONE, pairs of first and last shell). Zones beyond the twelfth are not mixed, without a message (the scan stops once J passes 24, lines 80–82↗), where CONVEC prints "TOO MANY MIX ZONES".

  2. Mixing. In each zone (one shell is enough at the centre), every species $k$ (11, or 15 with LEXCOM) becomes $$\bar X_k = \frac{\sum_j X_{k,j}\,\Delta m_j}{\sum_j \Delta m_j}.$$

  3. Clean-up, at every shell: $X \ge 0$; $Z \le 1 - X$; and ¹⁶O (row 9) at least $0.99\times10^{-3}\,(Z - Z_\mathrm{env,m})$, where $Z_\mathrm{env,m}$ = ZENVM is the envelope's metal fraction other than the CNO elements (set by STARIN: "Z OTHER THAN CNO CYCLE ELEMENTS", ZENV times the mixture's non-CNO share), so $Z - Z_\mathrm{env,m}$ is the CNO part of $Z$ and the floor keeps ¹⁶O at least about 0.1% of the CNO mass.

    Question 2 (lines 117–122↗). Helium after the clean-up. $X$ is floored and $Z$ capped, but $Y$ is not recomputed (as MIX does, lines 473–474↗), so $X+Y+Z+X_{^3\mathrm{He}}$ may differ from 1 afterwards. Is this restored elsewhere?

    Question 3 (line 121↗). The oxygen floor. HCOMP(9,I) = MAX(HCOMP(9,I), 0.99D-3*(HCOMP(3,I)-ZENVM)): What is this floor for, and where does $0.99\times10^{-3}$ come from? (Probably a guard for helium-burning regions, where $Z$ exceeds the envelope's; this is a guess.)

Suspected bugs, by priority

(a) may give wrong results; (b) may crash; (c) misbehaves silently (a wrong message, an ignored setting); (d) fragile: correct today, but breaks on a change or an unusual input.

  1. (d) The sentinel LCZ(M+1) is written past the model. (lines 65–67↗) LCZ(M+1) = .FALSE. writes into the caller's array one element past the model, and the scan reads it; the arrays are declared LCZ(JSON), so this is out of bounds when M = JSON, which HPOINT's check (MNEW+NPT.GT.JSON, line 401↗) allows. CONVEC does the same with LC(M+1) (line 87↗). Correct for every model with fewer than JSON = 5000 shells. Read from the code, not run. A possible fix: scan I = 1,M and, after the loop, close a zone still open at the surface (IF(LCC), with MXZONE(J+1) = M), so that nothing past M is written or read.
