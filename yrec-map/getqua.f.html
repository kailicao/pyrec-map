Purpose

GETQUA solves for the quadrupole moment (the $P_2$ part of the gravitational potential perturbation) of a rotating star, "using the Sweet (1950) formulation as given in Zahn (1992)" (its comment). The result is needed for the meridional circulation velocity.

How it fits

  • Who calls it: only SECULR.
  • Inputs: HD ($\log\rho$), HG (gravity $g$), HRU (radius, unlogged), OMEGA, M.
  • Outputs: in QUADRU_MOD: QUAD (the quadrupole term per shell) and GG (a copy of $g$), read by GETW and VCIRC.
  • Modules: QUADRU_MOD (output); TRIDI_MOD as workspace for the tridiagonal solver (shared with CCOEFT, CTRIDI, DCOEFT, MIXCOM, TRIDIA); constants from CONSTANTS_MOD; ISHORT from IOUNITS_MOD, for the error message.

The method

The comment states the equation $$\frac{1}{r}\frac{d^2}{dr^2}(r\phi) - \frac{6\phi}{r^2} - 4\pi G\rho\frac{d\rho}{dP}\,\phi = -\frac{4\pi G}{3}\frac{r^2}{g}\frac{d}{dr}\left(\rho\Omega^2\right),$$ and the code discretizes, per point, $$\phi'' + \frac{2}{r}\phi' - \frac{6}{r^2}\phi - \frac{4\pi G}{g}\frac{d\rho}{dr}\,\phi = -\frac{4\pi G\,r^2}{3g}\,\frac{d(\rho\Omega^2)}{dr},$$ with $\phi$ = QUAD (with $dP/dr = -\rho g$, the sign of the $d\rho/dr$ term differs from that of the comment's first form, which is a slip, below). The code uses the model's $d\rho/dr$ directly, which needs only hydrostatic equilibrium to stand for $\rho\,d\rho/dP$, not the ideal gas law the comment cites (lines 40–41↗) (settled from the code, Nov 9, 2026). Derivatives are centered on the smaller of the two neighboring intervals (weights FPL, FMI). Boundary conditions: $\phi \to 0$ at the center; at the surface, $\phi \propto r^{-3}$ outside the star ("D PHI/DR + 3 PHI/R = 0," lines 87–97↗), applied through a point outside, $\phi_{M+1} = (r_M/r_{M+1})^3\phi_M$ (FX), with $\tfrac{2}{r}\phi' = -6\phi/r^2$ added to $-6\phi/r^2$, hence the $-12/r^2$ of B(M). Outside the star ($\rho = 0$) the comment's own equation has the solutions $r^2$ and $r^{-3}$, so the decaying $r^{-3}$ of the surface condition is the right one, and the header's "VARIES AS 1/R**4" (lines 45–47↗) is a slip; the two terms that the comment says "CANCEL" (line 88↗) add up, as they should (settled from the code, Nov 9, 2026). The tridiagonal system is solved by the Thomas algorithm ("from Numerical Recipes"); the run stops on a zero pivot (exit status 1), with a message naming the zone, to the terminal and the .short file.

The comment's first form has the wrong sign. Linearizing Poisson's equation with $P' = -\rho\phi$ gives $\nabla^2\phi + 4\pi G\rho\,(d\rho/dP)\,\phi$, and with hydrostatic equilibrium, $dP/dr = -\rho g$, the last term is $-(4\pi G/g)(d\rho/dr)\,\phi$: the code's -C4PIG*DRHO/HG (lines 62–63↗; lines 79–80↗), and the sign of the comment's second form ("+4*PI*G*RHO*QUAD," lines 38–39↗, which lacks $d\rho/dP$). So the code is right, and the comment's first form, $-4\pi G\rho\,(d\rho/dP)\,\phi$ (line 36↗), has the sign slip (settled from the code, Nov 10, 2026).

At the first point, $d\rho/dr$ is divided by $\Delta r = \min(r_1, r_2 - r_1)$, not by $r_2 - r_1$ (suspected bug 2).

Question 1 (line 61↗; line 65↗). The central right-hand side. The first row's right-hand side D(1) uses $\Omega_1^2\,d\rho/dr$ instead of $d(\rho\Omega^2)/dr$. Is this an intended approximation near the center?
