Explanation

Purpose. EQSCVE interpolates the Saumon–Chabrier–Van Horn (SCV) equation of state in its table for the surface (envelope) composition. It returns the density, the thermodynamic derivatives and the ionization fractions of hydrogen and helium. For any other composition it hands the work to EQSCVG, which combines the pure-hydrogen, pure-helium and metal tables.

How it fits.

  • Who calls it: only EQSTAT2, when LSCV is set and $\log T <$ TSCUT, right after EQSAHA. If the point is outside the table (LCALC false), the routine returns without touching its outputs, so the EQSAHA values stand.
  • Inputs: TL, T, P, BETA, X, Z. It compares X, Z with XENV, ZENV from /COMP/.
  • What it calls: KSPLINE and KSPLINT (a four-point natural spline), INTERP (four-point Lagrange) and EQSCVG.
  • Outputs: DL, D, QDT, QDP, QCP, DELA, FXION, LCALC.
  • Modules: SCVEOS_MOD holds the tables (TLOGX, TABLENV for the envelope mixture, TABLEX, TABLEY, TABLEZ, SMIX, NPTSX) and the last indices IDTSCV, IDP; it is filled by SETSCV and used also by EQSCVG and SETUPS. The flag LSCV is now in NMLPAR_MOD. COMP_MOD gives the envelope composition; CONSTANTS_MOD the constants.

The method.

  1. The table is in $\log T$ and $\log P_\mathrm{gas}$, with $P_\mathrm{gas} = \beta P$. Out of table if $\log T$ is outside TLOGX, if $\log P_\mathrm{gas} < 4$, or if it is above the last pressure of the stencil's lowest-temperature row.
  2. Find the 4×4 stencil (IDTSCV, IDP), starting from the last one. For each of the 4 temperatures, a natural spline in $\log P_\mathrm{gas}$ gives five quantities (columns 2–6 of TABLENV: $\log\rho$, $Q_{\rho T}$, $Q_{\rho P}$, $c_P$, $\partial U/\partial T$); a second spline in $\log T$ gives their values at the point.
  3. Smoothing at stencil changes. When the point is within TOLST = 0.032 in $\log T$ (or TOLSP = 0.08 in $\log P$) of the edge of the middle interval, the routine also interpolates on the stencil shifted by one, and blends the two linearly (FST, FSP), so that the result does not jump when the stencil changes.

  4. Radiation. The tables hold the gas alone. With $Q_{\rho P,0}$ and $Q_{\rho T,0}$ from the table, $$Q_{\rho P} = \frac{Q_{\rho P,0}}{\beta},\qquad \chi_T = \beta\left(-\frac{Q_{\rho T,0}}{Q_{\rho P,0}}\right) + 4(1-\beta),\qquad Q_{\rho T} = -\chi_T\,Q_{\rho P},$$ $$\frac{\partial U}{\partial T} \mathrel{+}= \frac{4}{T}\cdot\frac{3P_\mathrm{rad}}{\rho},\qquad c_P = \frac{\partial U}{\partial T} - \frac{P}{\rho T}Q_{\rho T}\chi_T,\qquad \nabla_\mathrm{ad} = -\frac{P\,Q_{\rho T}}{\rho T\,c_P}.$$

  5. Ionization. From the pure-hydrogen table TABLEX and the pure-helium table TABLEY (columns 2 and 3, Lagrange interpolation on the same stencil and pressure abscissae as TABLENV), the particle fractions of H$_2$, H, He and He$^+$; charge and particle conservation give the rest, and $$x_{\mathrm{H}^+} = \frac{n_{\mathrm{H}^+}}{n_{\mathrm{H}_2} + n_\mathrm{H} + n_{\mathrm{H}^+}}, \quad x_{\mathrm{He}^+} = \frac{n_{\mathrm{He}^+}}{n_\mathrm{He} + n_{\mathrm{He}^+} + n_{\mathrm{He}^{++}}},$$ and likewise for He$^{++}$.

Questions for the experts

  1. H$_2$ counted once. In step 5, the denominator counts each H$_2$ molecule as one particle, but it holds two hydrogen nuclei. The comment says the result is "the fraction of hydrogen nuclei that are singly ionized". Should it be $2n_{\mathrm{H}_2}$?
  2. Mixed EOS outputs. When SCV succeeds, EQSTAT2 keeps RMU, EMU and the second derivatives from EQSAHA, while the density comes from SCV. So $\mu$ is no longer consistent with $\rho = \beta P\mu/(RT)$. Is that intended?
  3. One pressure grid for four temperatures. The pressure abscissae are always taken from the row IDTSCV (the lowest temperature of the stencil), also for the other three rows. The ionization step also uses TABLENV's pressures for TABLEX and TABLEY. That is right only if every row of all three tables has the same pressure grid. Is it so in the SCV tables?
  4. Unused column. $c_P$ from the table (TEMPT(4), QCP0) is interpolated but never used; $c_P$ is rebuilt from $\partial U/\partial T$. Can the interpolation be dropped?
  5. Spline and Lagrange. The thermodynamic quantities use splines, the ionization fractions Lagrange interpolation without the smoothing of step 3. Is the difference intended?
