Purpose

EQSAHA is the Yale equation of state for partially ionized gas: it solves the Saha equations for hydrogen, helium (two stages) and ten metals, and returns the density, the ionization fractions and the thermodynamic derivatives. The name "Prather EOS" in EQSTAT2's comments refers to it.

How it fits

  • Who calls it: only EQSTAT2, below the temperature TSCUT. It is called even when the SCV tables are used, since "this EOS replaces the call to EQSAHA, except for derivative purposes"; EQSCVE then overwrites what it can.
  • Inputs: FX, the number fractions of the twelve species (set by EQSTAT2 from FXENV in /COMP/); TL, PL; BETA and its relatives; AMU; LATMO (skip the derivatives, for atmospheres); LDERIV (also compute second derivatives).
  • Outputs: D, DL, EMU, RMU = $R(\mu_\mathrm{ion}^{-1}+\mu_e^{-1}) = \mathcal{R}/\mu$, the gas constant over the mean molecular weight, so that $P_\mathrm{gas} = \rho\,(\mathcal{R}/\mu)\,T$ (AMU $=\mu_\mathrm{ion}^{-1}$ and EMU $=\mu_e^{-1}$ are its two terms), FXION ($\mathrm{H^+}$, $\mathrm{He^+}$, $\mathrm{He^{++}}$ fractions), QDT, QDP, QCP, DELA and, with LDERIV, their derivatives. KSAHA is the caller's iteration counter, to which this call adds its iterations.
  • Modules: only CONSTANTS_MOD (with CMKH the constant term of the Saha equation) and IOUNITS_MOD (the unit ISHORT).

The method

  1. Saha constants. For each species $i$, with ionization temperature $\chi_i/k$ (SAHAT, in K: 59 630 for Na up to 250 200 for Ne; SAHET0 = 285 270 and SAHET1 = 631 370 for He and $\mathrm{He^+}$) and a constant $u_i$ (SAHAU), $$K_i = \frac{1}{\beta}\exp\!\left[\ln 10\,(u_i + 2.5\log T - \log P + C) - \frac{\chi_i}{kT}\right].$$ The species order is that of ATOMW2 in EQSTAT2: Na, Al, Mg, Fe, Si, C, H, O, N, Ar, Ne, He. The list is in order of increasing ionization temperature. The loop stops at the first species whose exponent is below $-40$: that species and all later ones are treated as neutral (so hydrogen, seventh, counts as ionized only if NZ0 $\ge 7$, and FXION(1) is 0 otherwise). A species whose exponent is above $+40$ gets $K_i = 10^{16}$ (fully ionized) and is left out of the derivative sums.

    Question 1 (lines 47–49↗). The constants $u_i$. SAHAU has no comment; they are probably the logarithms of the partition-function ratios. Could their source be cited? 2. Electron number. With $E$ the electrons per atom (E) and $\epsilon = E/(1+E)$, the ionized fraction of species $i$ (the ten metals and hydrogen) is $x_i = K_i/(K_i + \epsilon)$, and charge balance reads $$E = \sum_i f_i x_i + f_\mathrm{He}\,(x_1 + 2x_2),$$ with the helium fractions $x_1, x_2$ from their own Saha equations. The routine solves this coupled system by Newton iteration (at most 20 steps, tolerance $10^{-9}$), reducing the $3\times3$ Jacobian by hand; E and the helium fractions keep their values between calls as the next starting guess.

    Question 2 (line 94↗; lines 186–192↗). No-convergence path. After 20 iterations the routine writes "SAHA FAILURE" and continues with the unconverged $E$; the caller is not told. Is that acceptable, or should it be?

    Question 3 (line 53↗; line 55↗). State between calls. E, SAHEX1 and SAHEX2 persist between calls (SAVE and DATA). The result can then depend slightly on the order of calls (for example during the numerical derivatives in EQSTAT). Is that a concern at the tolerance used? 3. Density: $\mu_e^{-1} = E\,\mu_\mathrm{ion}^{-1}$ and $\rho = \beta P / [R(\mu_\mathrm{ion}^{-1} + \mu_e^{-1})T]$. 4. Derivatives. Differentiating the Saha system gives $\partial\ln E/\partial\ln T$ and $\partial\ln E/\partial\ln P$, then $Q_{\rho T}$, $Q_{\rho P}$, the energy derivative $\partial U/\partial T$ (including ionization energy), $c_P = \partial U/\partial T - (P/\rho T)Q_{\rho T}$ and $\nabla_\mathrm{ad} = -(P/\rho T)Q_{\rho T}/c_P$.

What it leaves out. The solver has no molecules ($\mathrm{H_2}$), no $\mathrm{H^-}$, no pressure ionization and no Coulomb terms, and treats the electrons as a classical gas. With the code's defaults (LOPALE06 and LSCV false) it nevertheless gives the equation of state of every layer below TSCUT, including the cool, dense envelopes of M dwarfs and brown dwarfs (whether that is adequate there, where molecules and degeneracy would be expected to matter, is the question below). The documentation calls OPAL 2006 "strongly recommended," and 45 of the 51 test-suite runs set LOPALE06 (47 set LSCV), so in practice this solver is used mainly outside those tables and for the second derivatives; whether the default should change is eqstat2.f, question 4.

64def eqsaha_saha_k(tl: float, pl: float, ti: float, betai: float, cln: float, cmkh: float, sahatt: np.ndarray,
65                  sahak: np.ndarray) -> tuple[float, int, int]:
66    """The Saha K's of the 11 species (loop 11, Fortran lines 63–77), in the order of increasing ionization
67    temperature.
68
69    The first species with an exponent below -STOL ends the list: it and all later ones count as neutral. Above
70    +STOL, the species is fully ionized (K = K_FULL) and left out of the derivative sums (NZ1).
71
72    Parameters
73    ----------
74    tl, pl : float
75        log10 T and log10 P.
76    ti : float
77        1/T.
78    betai : float
79        1/beta.
80    cln, cmkh : float
81        constants_mod's ln 10 and its Saha constant (module variables, passed in).
82    sahatt, sahak : np.ndarray, shape: `(NZ + 1,)`, dtype: ``float64``
83        Filled in place up to the first neutral species: chi/kT and K.
84
85    Returns
86    -------
87    stemp, nz0, nz1 : float, int, int
88        2.5 log T - log P + CMKH; the last species not neutral; the first not fully ionized (at most NZ).
89
90    """
91    stemp = 2.50 * tl - pl + cmkh
92    nz1 = 1
93    for i in range(1, NZ + 1):
94        sahatt[i] = SAHAT[i] * ti
95        sahak[i] = cln * (SAHAU[i] + stemp) - sahatt[i]
96        if sahak[i] < -STOL:
97            break                                  # go to 12
98        if sahak[i] > +STOL:
99            sahak[i] = K_FULL                      # label 10
100            nz1 = i + 1
101            continue
102        sahak[i] = betai * math.exp(sahak[i])
103    else:
104        i = NZ + 1
105    nz0 = i - 1                                    # label 12
106    nz1 = min(NZ, nz1)
107    return stemp, nz0, nz1
