Purpose

This file runs the Henyey iterations that correct the interior model at one level of the time step: it sets the surface boundary condition from the envelope, mixes and burns the composition, builds the linearized structure equations, solves them for the corrections to $\log P$, $\log T$, $\log r$ and $L$, applies the corrections (damped), and decides whether the model has converged.

How it fits

  • Inputs: the arguments: the model (HP, HT, HR, HL, HD, HS, HS1, HS2, HCOMP, HPP, HTT), DELTS (time step, s), NITER (the maximum number of iterations), ITLVL (the level of iteration within the step), the flags LNEW, LRESET, LSBC, the envelope triangle (envelopes integrated at three points around the model's $(\log T_\mathrm{eff}, \log L)$, whose values at the fitting point are fitted by a plane to give the outer boundary condition; SURFBC: TRIT, TRIL, PS, TS, RS, FTRI, ISTORE, ESTORE), rotation (FP, FT, OMEGA, HJM, …), and CMIXL (the mixing length, set by main.f from the namelist's CMIXLA, passed on to SURFBC, MIX and COEFFT); from NMLPAR_MOD, the tolerances HTOLER, the damping settings FCORR0, FCORRI, and the options LKUTHE (the helium flash), LCORR (print corrections), LROT, LDH, LEXCOM; the current damping FCORR (CTOL_MOD); the start-of-step composition HCOMPP and, with rotation, the convective flags LCZO that HPOINT (or STARIN, for the starting model) stored from OVROT (OLDMOD_MOD), XNEW, ZNEW (COMP_MOD), the Debye–Hückel composition (DEBHU_MOD), and the output units from IOUNITS_MOD.
  • Who calls it: the main program (main.f), within the evolution loop.
  • What it calls: SURFBC (the surface boundary condition from envelope integrations, as fitting coefficients CFENV), MIX (burning and mixing over the step), COEFFT (the equations and their derivatives, reduced by REDUCE), HSOLVE (the back-substitution), and with rotation GETROT and FPFT.
  • Outputs: the corrected model, BL, TEFFL, LFINI (converged), LARGE (corrections too large: main.f then calls STARIN, which stops the run; see main.f, step 2 of the evolution step), ITDONE, HMAX/NHMAX (largest corrections and where), TLUMX (luminosity by source, renormalized), and with rotation FP, FT, OMEGA, HKEROT. With LCORR, a line per iteration to ISHORT.

The method

The system as a whole. The unknowns are the corrections $\delta\log P$, $\delta\log T$, $\delta\log r$ and $\delta L$ at each of the $M$ points, $4M$ in all; the composition is not among them (MIX sets it: main.f's call before levels 1 and 2, and step 2 at levels 3 and 4). They are fixed by $4(M-1)$ difference equations, one for each structure equation between each pair of neighbouring points; two central conditions at point 1 (COEFFT); and two surface conditions, the envelope fit below (HSRF). The matrix is block-bidiagonal, and REDUCE and HSOLVE are the forward and backward sweeps that solve it.

  1. If LSBC, call SURFBC with the envelope composition. It returns the fit of the envelope's base as linear relations in $\log P$ and $\log T$ at the outermost interior point: $$\log r = c_1\log P + c_2\log T + c_3, \qquad \log L = c_4\log P + c_5\log T + c_6, \qquad \log T_\mathrm{eff} = c_7\log P + c_8\log T + c_9.$$ With LDH, the composition for the Debye–Hückel correction (XXDH, YYDH, ZZDH, ZDH) is first set from shell M.
  2. At ITLVL > 2 with $\Delta t > 0$: reset the composition to its start-of-step value and call MIX. The call to OVROT that followed is commented out (lines 162–165↗); with rotation, OVROT runs in HPOINT after each rezone ("CALCULATE OVERSHOOT", lines 779–781↗) and in STARIN for the starting model (lines 1032–1033↗), and CRRECT uses the flags they store, LCZO (settled from the code, Nov 9, 2026).
  3. If the boundary condition was recomputed, the triangle did not flip (LFLP from SURFBC) and the model had converged at the previous level (LFINI), return at once.
  4. Iterate (at most NITER times):
  5. COEFFT and REDUCE build the system. Renormalize TLUMX so that its sum equals $L$ at the surface (not during the helium flash).

  6. From the second iteration, if the largest residuals of the equations (HMAX from REDUCE) are below HTOLER(5,·), the model has converged.

    Question 1 (lines 190–201↗). Residual test before solving. At iteration 2 and later, if the residuals are below HTOLER(5,·), the routine returns as converged without solving. The comment notes that boundary equations are not checked. Can the surface condition then be unmet? (The message prints ITDONE+1 as the iteration count, but ITDONE is not incremented on this path.)

  7. The surface rows from the fit above: HSRF(1..3) linearize the $\log r$ relation and HSRF(4..6) the $\log L$ relation, multiplied by $\ln 10\,L$ since $L$, not $\log L$, is the unknown; the $T_\mathrm{eff}$ relation only updates TEFFL afterwards. HSOLVE gives the corrections $\delta$.

  8. Converged if $\max|\delta\log P|$, $\max|\delta\log T|$, $\max|\delta\log r|$ and $\max \min(|\delta L|, |\delta L/L|)$ are all below HTOLER(·,1); stop (return with LARGE) if any exceeds HTOLER(·,2).

    Question 2 (line 234↗). The luminosity test. The convergence test on $L$ uses $\min(|\delta L|, |\delta L/L|)$, with HL in solar units: where $L > 1\,L_\odot$ this is the relative change, elsewhere the absolute one (in a star fainter than the Sun, the absolute one everywhere); the first shell enters with $|\delta L|$ alone (line 215↗). Is the mixed test intended?

  9. Apply $x \leftarrow x + f\,\delta x$, with the damping $f$ = FCORR, raised by FCORRI per iteration up to 1 if FCORR0 > 0; during the helium flash $f = 0.8$ once $|\delta L| \le 0.5$ and 1 once $\le 5\times10^{-3}$. The changes of $\log P$, $\log T$ over the step (HPP, HTT) are updated too.

  10. New $\log L$ and $\log T_\mathrm{eff}$ (from the fit); with rotation, new $\Omega$, $f_P$, $f_T$ and rotational kinetic energy $\tfrac12\Omega j\,\Delta m$. GETROT gets the flags LCZO, so that it imposes the convection-zone rotation law on each convection zone as a whole (WCZIMP) and inverts the other shells one by one.

From YREC's documentation

Physics Options (Numerical convergence options):

variable default meaning (documentation)
HTOLER(1..4,1) 6.0D-5, 4.5D-5, 3.0D-5, 9.0D-5 convergence tolerances for $\log P$, $\log T$, $\log R$, $L/L_\mathrm{tot}$: the model has converged when the largest correction anywhere is below all four (all local)
HTOLER(5,1), HTOLER(5,2) 3.0D-5, 2.5D-6 if analytic and numerical derivatives agree better than this ($P$ and $R$; $T$ and $L$), the iteration is skipped
HTOLER(1..4,2) 0.9, 0.5, 0.5, 2.0 abort thresholds: a correction larger than this is a divergence (rarely used)
FCORR0 0.8 the predicted corrections can be overestimated at first; the first iteration multiplies them by this
FCORRI 0.1 the increase of that factor at each iteration (with the defaults, 0.8, 0.9, 1.0); larger is faster but less stable
LCORR true write the details of convergence to the .short file

The code does not compare derivatives for HTOLER(5,·): it compares the largest residuals of the difference equations (HMAX, from REDUCE) with HTOLER(5,1) for $P$ and $r$ and HTOLER(5,2) for $T$ and $L$, and returns as converged ("R.H.S. BELOW TOLERANCES") without solving (step 4).

Question 3 (lines 190–200↗). HTOLER(5,·) in the documentation. The Physics namelist page says that if "analytic and numerical derivatives agree better than" HTOLER(5,1), HTOLER(5,2), "the iteration is skipped". The code ("CHECK ON SIGNIFICANCE OF R.H.S. EQUATIONS", message "R.H.S. BELOW TOLERANCES") compares the largest residuals of the difference equations, HMAX, with them, HTOLER(5,1) for $P$ and $r$ and HTOLER(5,2) for $T$ and $L$; no numerical derivatives are formed. Should the documentation describe the residual test?

Suspected bugs, by priority

(a) may give wrong results; (b) may crash; (c) misbehaves silently (a wrong message, an ignored setting); (d) fragile: correct today, but breaks on a change or an unusual input.

  1. (a) JENV is unset at levels 1 and 2. (line 57↗; lines 150–161↗; lines 176–179↗) JENV, the first shell of the convective envelope, is a local of CRRECT, set only by its own call to MIX at levels 3 and 4 (ITLVL > 2), and passed to COEFFT. COEFFT reads it when the entropy terms are on and mass is gained (LMDOT and DMDT0 > 0): shells with IM.GE.JENV get the extra changes DLOGT, DLOGP in $\log T$, $\log P$ (lines 324–331↗). At levels 1 and 2 of the first step, JENV has never been set; at levels 1 and 2 of later steps it is the value from the previous step's MIX, not the one the main program's own MIX and FINDSH have just computed (main.f keeps its own JENV). What follows: with accretion, the extra terms go to the wrong shells (or, with an unset value, to all or none) until level 3. Only with LMDOT (off by default) and DMDT0 > 0 (the default is $-10^{-14}$, mass loss). Read from the code, not run; whether level 3 removes the effect was not traced. A possible fix: pass the main program's JENV (set by STARIN, MIX and FINDSH) to CRRECT as an argument, in place of the local, so that CRRECT's MIX at levels 3 and 4 updates the same variable.
