Purpose

This file integrates the outer layers of the star, which the interior model does not contain: first the atmosphere, from small optical depth to the photosphere, then the envelope, from the photosphere down to the interior's outermost mass point. The result is the pressure, temperature and radius at that fitting point, which SURFBC turns into the surface boundary condition of the interior; the run of the envelope is stored for output, the acoustic depth and the convective overturn time.

How it fits

  • Inputs: the arguments B ($L$), TEFFL, GL ($\log g$), RL ($\log R$), HSTOT ($\log M$), X, Z, FPL, FTL (rotational corrections), PLIM (the expected end pressure), IE (which point of the envelope triangle), LSBC, LPRT, LPULPT, and CMIXL (the mixing-length constant, passed on to QENV through QENVMIX_MOD, since QENV is called through BSSTEP with a fixed argument list). From NMLPAR_MOD: the atmosphere choice KTTAU (0 Eddington gray, 1 Krishna Swamy, 2 the HRA $T$–$\tau$ relation, 3 Kurucz, 4 Allard, 5 Castelli–Kurucz 2004); the integration controls (ATMERR, ATMD0, ATMBEG, … and ENVERR, ENVBEG, …); TENV0, TENV1, from which TENV = (TENV0 + TENV1)/2 is computed here; LCALCENV, LNEWTCZ; the output switches (LSTATM, LSTENV, LSTCH, IPVER). Also STOLR0 (INTPAR_MOD), SENV ($\log$ of the fitting point's mass fraction, COMP_MOD), HRAS (ATMOS_MOD), LCLCD (ACDPTH_MOD), the constants (CONSTANTS_MOD) and the unit numbers (IOUNITS_MOD).
  • Who calls it: SURFBC (the boundary condition), CALCAD, GETNEWENV, GETTAU, STARIN, STITCH and WRTMOD.
  • What it calls: for tabulated atmospheres SURFP, KCSURFP or ALSURFP; for gray ones HRA, EQSTAT and GETOPAC; for both integrations BSSTEP with the derivative routines QATM and QENV; at the end TAUCAL.
  • Outputs:
  • arguments: PS(IE), TS(IE), RS(IE) (the fitting point's $\log P$, $\log T$, $\log r$), ESTORE, ISTORE (the state where $T$ first reaches TENV), all set only when LSBC; counters KATM, KENV, KSAHA;
  • ENVSTRUCT_MOD: the envelope profile ($\log P$, $\log T$, $\log m$, $\log\rho$, $\log r$, $X$, $Z$, convective flag, gradients, velocities, $\beta$, $\Gamma_1$, $c_P$, ionization, opacity, $L$, $\rho_T$), reordered from the centre outward; read by CALCAD, GETNEWENV, GETTAU, STARIN, STITCH, TAUINT;
  • ATMSTRUCT_MOD (the atmosphere profile, for STITCH); PPHOT (OVRTRN_MOD, the photospheric pressure, for AMCALC); LPUMOD (PULSE1_MOD, whether QATM and QENV compute the pulsation values) and QQED, QQET, QFS (PULSE2_MOD);
  • text: the atmosphere and envelope tables to ISTOR, summaries to ISHORT, pulsation files IOPATM, IOPENV.

The atmosphere.

  • Tables (KTTAU = 3, 4, 5): the routine reads $\log P$ (and $\log T$) at the photosphere from the table; if the Allard table fails, it switches to the gray atmosphere: it sets KTTAU = 0 and LTTAU, and integrates the gray atmosphere at once (lines 241–254↗). The switch is not for good: at its next call SURFBC switches back to the Allard tables, with a new triangle, as soon as $\log T_\mathrm{eff}$ is below the table's top (lines 109–128↗; settled from the code, Nov 9, 2026).

    Question 1 (lines 244–254↗; lines 120–127↗). A failure in $\log g$. ALSURFP also fails when $\log g$ is outside the table at a $T_\mathrm{eff}$ inside it (lines 162–172↗, lines 225–237↗). SURFBC's switch back tests only $T_\mathrm{eff}$, so such a star would go back to the Allard tables at each call of SURFBC, fail again in ENVINT, and start a new triangle each time (not traced in a run). Should the switch back also test $\log g$, or should a failure in $\log g$ stop the run?

  • Gray (KTTAU = 0, 1, 2): start at a small optical depth, with the temperature from the $T$–$\tau$ relation at $\tau \to 0$ (Eddington: $\log T = \log T_\mathrm{eff} - \tfrac14\log\tfrac43 + \tfrac14\log\tfrac23$, that is $T^4 = T_\mathrm{eff}^4/2$; Krishna Swamy: $T^4 = \tfrac34 T_\mathrm{eff}^4 \times 0.550$; HRA: its own value at $\tau = 2/3$ shifted by HRAS) and the pressure $P = \rho\mathcal{R}T + aT^4/3$ at a chosen density ATMD0 (the gas term with $\mu$ taken as 1, as the code writes CGAS*ATMDX*T; it only sets the starting point: "The integration starts at an arbitrary low density", YREC paper §2.3.6). The starting optical depth is $\tau_0 = \kappa P/g$, where $\kappa$ is the opacity at that $P$ and $T$ (X0 = PL - GL + DLOG10(O), line 291↗; whether this is meant as the optical depth of the column above the starting layer is question 3). Then, with $\log\tau$ as the variable, $$\frac{d\log P}{d\log\tau} = \frac{g\,\tau}{\kappa P},$$ with $g$ multiplied by the rotational factor $f_P$ (FPL) in QATM's slopes and in the starting slope DYDX(1) alike, up to $\tau = 2/3$ (Eddington and HRA; $\tau = 0.312$ for Krishna Swamy, the depth where its $T = T_\mathrm{eff}$). The tables, too, give $P$ at $\tau = 2/3$ (YREC paper, §2.3.6), so every option ends where $T = T_\mathrm{eff}$ and the envelope below starts at $r = R$ from $L = 4\pi R^2\sigma T_\mathrm{eff}^4$; switching KTTAU changes the photospheric pressure, not the radius. If the starting $\tau$ lies beyond the end, the density is divided by 10 and the start retried, with no limit on the retries (lines 367–372↗; suspected bug 5).

    Question 2 (lines 280–281↗; lines 367–372↗). ATMD0 a logarithm? The documentation calls ATMD0 the "log10 of starting density at τ = 0" (docs/source/namelist_physics.md, line 143), but ENVINT uses it as the density itself (PL = log10((CGAS*ATMDX + CA3*T**3)*T), and divides it by 10 to retry), and the default, 1.0D-10, is a density in g cm⁻³. Should the documentation say "starting density"?

    Question 3 (lines 283–291↗). The starting optical depth. The integration starts at $\tau_0 = \kappa P/g$. If the pressure there is the weight of the column above, $P = g\Sigma$, this is $\kappa\Sigma$, the optical depth of that column with $\kappa$ taken as constant: is that the reasoning, and does the start's error matter once the integration reaches $\tau = 2/3$?

The envelope. With $\log P$ as the variable and $y = (\log(m/M), \log T, \log r)$, QENV gives $$\frac{d\log m}{d\log P} = -\frac{4\pi r^4 P}{G m^2 f_P}, \qquad \frac{d\log T}{d\log P} = \nabla, \qquad \frac{d\log r}{d\log P} = -\frac{P r f_P}{G m \rho},$$ where $f_P$ is the rotational distortion factor (FPL); note that it multiplies $d\log r/d\log P$ where consistency with the interior equations asks for $1/f_P$ (suspected bug 1). The integration starts at the photosphere, with $\log(m/M) = 0$, $r = R$ (RL), and the photospheric $T$ and $P$ from the atmosphere or the table, and runs with BSSTEP until $\log(m/M)$ reaches SENV; the step is shortened or reversed to land on it, and, when LSBC is false, the first step past PLIM is cut to end on it. The end point is interpolated linearly to SENV. On the way down, when LSBC, the state where $\log T$ first reaches TENV (6.0 with the defaults TENV0 = 3, TENV1 = 9) is kept in ESTORE ($\log P$, $\log T$, $\log r$, $\log(m/M)$) and ISTORE (the vertex), for HPOINT. TENV0 and TENV1 bound the fitting point's temperature: HPOINT deletes the outer interior points cooler than TENV0 (moving SENV inward), and, using ESTORE, adds a new outermost interior point, at SENV = ESTORE(4), when the outermost point has grown hotter than TENV1. The defaults are 3 and 9; all 51 test-suite runs use 2 and 9, and their input files' comment calls the bounds a failsafe, while the documentation says "Code stops below this" and "above this" (whether they are only safeguards is question 4). If the envelope mass is negligible (SENV > -1e-12), the photosphere is the fitting point. In the convection zone the loop also sums $\Delta r/v$ into TAUCZENV (line 627↗, lines 783–791↗), which is never stored or returned: unused?.

Question 4 (lines 805–807↗; HPOINT). TENV0 and TENV1. HPOINT deletes outer interior points cooler than TENV0 and adds one when the outermost point is hotter than TENV1; the documentation describes them as limits at which the "code stops". Are they meant only as safeguards that a normal run never reaches, and is the test suite's 2 (against the default 3) a deliberate choice?

After the integration. The profile is reversed (when SENV $\le -10^{-12}$, the complement of the test that skips the integration); for TAUCAL the masses are passed in grams (ENVS1) and as differences in units of the total mass (ENVS2, used only as weights in an average, so the unit cancels); the base of the convection zone is found by linear interpolation of $\nabla_\mathrm{rad} - \nabla$ (its radius, ENVCZL, is computed but no longer kept or used: unused?, although the comment after it says "ENVCZL above is still used below", lines 950–953↗), and TAUCAL computes the overturn time (unless LNEWTCZ, true by default; with rotation it reads the gravity ENVG, never set: suspected bug 1). With LCALCENV false, the routine stops after the atmosphere, having set PPHOT.

Design

The routine mixes physics with output for several purposes (the .store tables, pulsation files, acoustic-depth arrays), and each output path keeps its own copies of $\Gamma_1$ and related values. Failure to finish in 2000 steps stops the run, with exit status 1 (STOP 1). LDERIV is set by the pulsation test and then forced to .TRUE. on the next line. The atmosphere's two sets of arrays for STITCH stay in step: ATMOP, ATMOT, … are stored when LPRT.AND.LSTATM (lines 403–425↗), ATMOR, ADELS and NUMATM when LCLCD, LSTCH or pulsation output is on (lines 433–475↗), and STITCH calls ENVINT with LPRT = .TRUE. (line 160↗) and writes the atmosphere only when LSTATM (line 202↗), so with LSTCH both sets come from the same steps (settled from the code, Nov 9, 2026).

65def _pulse_lines(unit, values, per_line):
66    """Write values to a pulsation file, per_line to a line (FORMATs 5001:
67    5E16.9 and 6001: 5E23.16, as 5, 5, 5, 4; 6003: 6E23.16, as 6, 6, 6,
68    4). Python's E format prints d.ddd where Fortran's prints 0.ddd."""
69
70    width, digits = (16, 9) if per_line == 0 else (23, 16)
71    per_line = per_line or 5
72    lines = []
73    for k in range(0, len(values), per_line):
74        lines.append("".join(f"{v:{width}.{digits}E}"
75                             for v in values[k:k + per_line]))
76    iounits_mod.write(unit, "\n".join(lines))

673def _store_envelope_point(n, x, z, b):
674    """Copy the point just integrated (envprt_mod, pulse2_mod) into entry n
