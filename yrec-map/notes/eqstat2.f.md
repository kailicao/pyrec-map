## Explanation

**Purpose.** `EQSTAT2` is the core of YREC's equation of state: it chooses among, and blends, the Yale Saha solver, the relativistic degenerate gas, the SCV tables and the OPAL 2006 table, depending on temperature, density and the run's settings. Its header calls it "the old EOS routine"; since 2006 the code calls it through [EQSTAT](eqstat.f.md#eqstat).

**How it fits.**
- **Who calls it:** `EQSTAT` (once or five times per call, see there) and [CALCAD](calcad.f.md#calcad) directly.
- **Inputs:** `TL`, `PL`, `X`, `Z`, `LDERIV`, `LATMO`; `DL` as a starting guess.
- **What it calls:** [EQSAHA](eqsaha.f.md#eqsaha) (Saha ionization), [EQSCVE](eqscve.f.md#eqscve) (SCV tables), [EQRELV](eqrelv.f.md#eqrelv) (fully ionized, degenerate gas), and for OPAL [OEQOS06](oeqos06.f.md#oeqos06) with [EQBOUND06](eqbound06.f.md#eqbound06) (the OPAL 1995 and 2001 tables, with `OEQOS`, `EQBOUND`, `OEQOS01` and `EQBOUND01`, removed since `rp_1`).
- **Outputs:** as listed in `EQSTAT`: $`T`$, $`P`$, $`\rho`$, $`\beta`$, the ionization fractions, $`\mu`$ terms, $`\eta`$, $`Q_{\rho T}`$, $`Q_{\rho P}`$, $`c_P`$, $`\nabla_\mathrm{ad}`$ and their derivatives.
- **Modules:** `NMLPAR_MOD` (the namelist settings: the flag `LOPALE06`, `LSCV`, `TSCUT`, the temperature below which Saha is solved, and `LDIFZ`, metal diffusion); `COMP_MOD` (the envelope composition `XENV`, `ZENV`, `AMUENV`, `FXENV`, set in [STARIN](starin.f.md#starin)); `CONSTANTS_MOD` (constants: $`\ln 10`$, $`R`$ as `CGAS`, $`a/3`$ as `CA3`); `IOUNITS_MOD` (`ISHORT`).

**The method.**
1. **Composition.** `AMU` $`=\sum_i X_i/A_i`$ (ions per unit mass, in units of $`1/m_\mathrm{H}`$) and the number fractions `FX` of 12 species (Na, Al, Mg, Fe, Si, C, H, O, N, Ar, Ne, He; atomic weights in `ATOMW2`) are rescaled from the envelope values. Without metal diffusion, any change in $`Z`$ is taken as carbon ("ASSUME EXCESS Z(METALS) IS IN THE FORM OF CARBON(12)"); with it, all metals scale together by $`Z/Z_\mathrm{env}`$.
2. **Radiation.** $`1-\beta = aT^4/(3P)`$; then `BETA14` is redefined as $`4(1-\beta)/\beta`$ (with $`1-\beta`$ capped at 1). This is the radiation term of the gas's response to temperature: for an ideal gas with radiation, $`Q_{\rho T} = -(4-3\beta)/\beta = -1 - 4(1-\beta)/\beta`$, and [EQSAHA](eqsaha.f.md#eqsaha) builds its $`Q_{\rho T}`$ (`QDT = -1 - BETA14 - …`, the last term from ionization), $`c_P`$ and their derivatives on it. After an OPAL result `BETA14` is set to $`1-\beta`$ instead ([line 334](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqstat2.f#L334), [line 348](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqstat2.f#L348)), which would be wrong in these formulas, but no caller reads the `BETA14` it gets back: `EQSAHA` receives it within this call, before the OPAL step, and the callers of `EQSTAT` and `EQSTAT2` only declare it and pass it on (settled from the code, Nov 9, 2026: [line 165](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqstat2.f#L165), [lines 182–184](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqstat2.f#L182-L184), [lines 311–358](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqstat2.f#L311-L358)).

    > **Question** ([lines 159–164](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqstat2.f#L159-L164)). **Negative $`\beta`$.** When radiation pressure exceeds $`P`$, the routine writes a message and continues with $`\beta \lt{} 0`$ (so `BETAI` and `BETA14` are negative too). Should it stop, or tell the caller?

3. **Fully ionized gas** ($`\log T \ge`$ `TSCUT`): [EQRELV](eqrelv.f.md#eqrelv) alone, with $`\mu_e^{-1} = 0.9921X + 0.4995(1-X)`$ and $`R/\mu = R(\mu_\mathrm{ion}^{-1} + \mu_e^{-1})`$.
4. **Partial ionization** ($`\log T \lt{}`$ `TSCUT`): `EQSAHA`, and if `LSCV` is set, `EQSCVE`, which overwrites $`\rho`$ and the first derivatives where its table covers the point. A block after it would compute the SCV second derivatives numerically, but `LSCVD` is set to false just before it, so it never runs: unused? ([lines 189–241](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqstat2.f#L189-L241); see bug 1).

    Where SCV supplies $`\rho`$, `RMU`, `EMU` and the second derivatives still come from `EQSAHA` ([lines 181–187](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqstat2.f#L181-L187); [eqsaha.f, lines 194–195](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqsaha.f#L194-L195)), so below the transition band $`\mathcal{R}/\mu`$ is not $`\beta P/(\rho T)`$ there: [EQSCVE's question 1](eqscve.f.md#q1).

5. **Transition band** (`TSCUT` − 0.5 < $`\log T`$ < `TSCUT`): `EQRELV` is also called, and the two are blended with a smooth step. With $`w = 2(\mathrm{TSCUT} - \log T)`$, between 0 and 1,
   
   $$\displaystyle W(w) = w^3(6w^2 - 15w + 10),\qquad q = q\_\mathrm{rel} + W\\,(q\_\mathrm{Saha} - q\_\mathrm{rel})$$
   
   for $`\log\rho`$, the ionization fractions and the derivatives; then $`R/\mu = \beta P/(\rho T)`$ and $`\mu_e^{-1}`$ follows. Two gaps in the blend of the second derivatives are known to the developers (`KNOWN_ISSUES.md`, section 40, "EOS second derivatives"): The differences are not formed for `QDTT` and `QDTP`, so they come out as $`q_\mathrm{rel} + W\,q_\mathrm{Saha}`$ ([lines 255–256](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqstat2.f#L255-L256), [lines 277–285](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqstat2.f#L277-L285), [lines 298–299](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqstat2.f#L298-L299)), and the term from $`dW/d\log T`$ is left out (`WT1`, commented out, [line 276](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqstat2.f#L276)). The same section records that the converged models are unaffected; what suffers is the rate and robustness of the Newton iteration.
6. **OPAL.** If `LOPALE06` is set and the point is in the table's rough range ($`1870 \le T \le 2\times10^8\,\mathrm{K}`$, $`\log\rho \le 7`$), OPAL's values replace the above, or are blended with them by the weight $`f`$ from `EQBOUND06`:
   
   $$\displaystyle q = q\_\mathrm{Yale} + f\\,(q\_\mathrm{OPAL} - q\_\mathrm{Yale})$$
   
   for $`\log\rho`$, $`\beta`$, the $`\mu`$ terms, $`Q_{\rho T}`$, $`Q_{\rho P}`$, $`c_P`$ and $`\nabla_\mathrm{ad}`$. OPAL gives no second derivatives, so those stay from the Yale/SCV result. If OPAL fails (alternate return) or the point is out of its table, the routine exits with the Yale/SCV result. At the end the routine restores `Z` from its copy `Z0` ([line 91](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqstat2.f#L91), [line 362](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqstat2.f#L362)), but nothing changes `Z`: no statement here, and neither `OEQOS06` nor `EQSCVE` (or `EQSCVG`) assigns it (settled from the code, Nov 9, 2026: [oeqos06.f, line 91](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/oeqos06.f#L91), [oeqos06.f, line 122](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/oeqos06.f#L122)): unused?

    > **Question** ([lines 311–312](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqstat2.f#L311-L312)). **Hard-coded OPAL range.** The rough range ($`1870 \le T \le 2\times10^8\,\mathrm{K}`$, $`\log\rho \le 7`$) is hard-coded; the real edges are checked in `EQBOUND06`. Is this range still needed, and is it right for the 2006 table?

    > **Question** ([lines 117–152](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqstat2.f#L117-L152); [lines 344–355](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqstat2.f#L344-L355)). **The OPAL table's heavy-element mixture.** The Yale EOS takes its 12 species from the envelope's `FXENV`, with any excess $`Z`$ as carbon; the OPAL 2006 table (`FOPALE06`, `EOSOPAL06Z0.016492` in the documentation's example) is computed for one fixed mixture. In the ramp the two are averaged. Which mixture is the OPAL table for, and do the `FX` fractions match it? If not, the ramp blends two compositions; the effect on $`P`$ is small (the documentation notes that $`P`$ hardly depends on the metals), but it would show in $`\nabla_\mathrm{ad}`$ and $`c_P`$ across the ramp.


**From YREC's documentation.** [Microphysics in YREC](https://yrec.readthedocs.io/en/latest/microphysics.html) describes the layers: OPAL (`LOPALE06`, the 2006 tables, Rogers & Nayfonov [2002](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ApJ...576.1064R), valid for $`3.3 \lt{} \log T \lt{} 8.3`$ with a density edge; "strongly recommended," although off by default), then SCV (`LSCV`, from three tables for pure H, He and metals, combined in a thermodynamically consistent way; the metal table was made with YREC's own EOS), then YREC's own EOS, with a ramp near the edge of the OPAL tables. With both on, SCV is used only outside OPAL; a full SCV calculation needs OPAL off and SCV on. OPAL assumes a fixed heavy-element mixture, so settling does not change $`Z`$ in it (a minor effect, since $`P`$ hardly depends on the metals). YREC's own EOS assumes full ionization above $`\log T`$ = `TSCUT` (default 6.0); the documentation warns not to change it, since the Saha equation for H and He fails at high density. YREC's semi-analytic derivatives are used for the structure equations even where a table gives the pressure.

> **Question** ([nmlpar_mod.f, line 122](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/nmlpar_mod.f#L122)). **The documentation's removed options.** The microphysics page (`docs/source/microphysics.md`, lines 29–51) still describes `LOPALE`, `LOPALE01` and `FOPALE` (the 1996 and 2002 OPAL EOS) and the MHD EOS (`FMHD1`–`FMHD7`), all removed from the code since `rp_1`; it calls `LOPALE06` "strongly recommended" and says "We should alter the defaults to make LOPALE06 = TRUE," while its default is still false. Should the page be updated, and the default changed?

**What the code uses.** The documentation's last sentence holds for the second derivatives only: where OPAL applies, its own first derivatives ($`Q_{\rho T}`$, $`Q_{\rho P}`$, $`c_P`$, $`\nabla_\mathrm{ad}`$) replace or are blended with the Yale ones (step 6), and SCV's replace them where its tables cover the point (step 4). Beyond OPAL's range ($`T \gt{} 2\times10^8\,\mathrm{K}`$ or $`\log\rho \gt{} 7`$) only `EQRELV` applies: full ionization, relativistic degenerate electrons, and, as its one Coulomb term, the Debye–Hückel correction when `LDH` is set (as in every test-suite run; see [EQRELV](eqrelv.f.md#eqrelv) for its defaults). Of the 51 test-suite runs, 45 set `LOPALE06` and 47 `LSCV`.

> **Question** ([lines 167–177](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqstat2.f#L167-L177); [eqrelv.f, lines 179–185](https://github.com/yreclab/yrec/blob/7be4133/src/eqrelv.f#L179-L185)). **The EOS beyond OPAL's range.** Above $`2\times10^8\,\mathrm{K}`$ or $`\log\rho \gt{} 7`$ only `EQRELV` applies, whose only non-ideal term is Debye–Hückel, a weak-coupling correction. Is that adequate for the advanced stages the test suite runs (the runs to the end of core helium burning, `…_TAHB`), where a reader would expect a strong-coupling (ion-sphere or one-component-plasma) term?

**Changed since `rp_1`.** The OPAL 1995 and 2001 equations of state are removed (with their flags and calls), leaving OPAL 2006 alone; COMMON blocks to modules (`TSCUT`, `LDIFZ`, `LSCV` and `LOPALE06` now from `NMLPAR_MOD`; the unused `/CTLIM/`, `/DEBHU/` and `/SCVEOS/` declarations dropped), `IMPLICIT NONE`, and the local `FMU` renamed `FMUENV`.

### EQSTAT2

Chooses and blends the equations of state by regime (method above): composition, radiation, Saha or SCV below `TSCUT`, the relativistic gas above it with a smooth-step blend between, and OPAL 2006 on top where its table covers the point.
