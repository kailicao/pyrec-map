Purpose

KEMCOM solves the nuclear burning network for one shell, or one convection zone, over a time step: it finds the new mass fractions of H, ³He, ⁴He, ¹²C, ¹³C, ¹⁴N and ¹⁶O simultaneously and implicitly, by Newton iteration, from the reaction rates computed in ENGEB (its comment: "Don VandenBerg's notes explain what the SR is doing"). ¹⁸O is updated explicitly afterwards.

How it fits

  • Who calls it: MIX and ROTMIX, for each radiative shell (IBEGIN = IEND) and each convection zone (IBEGIN < IEND).
  • Inputs: HT ($\log T$), the rates HR1–HR8, HR10–HR12 and the branching ratio HF1 (per Gyr per amu, without the composition factors, as its comment says), HS2 (shell masses), DDAGE (the step in years); the abundances at the start of the step HCOMPP from the module OLDMOD_MOD.
  • Output: the new HCOMP (elements 1–7, 9 and 11; element 3, $Z$, is either reset or set to its zone mean, see step 4) for shells IBEGIN..IEND.
  • Calls: SIMEQC, the linear solver for the 7×8 Newton system.
  • Modules (COMMON blocks until rp_1): NMLPAR_MOD for the settings CMIN, ABSTOL, RELTOL, KEMMAX (formerly /BURTOL/, set by PARMIN) and TCUT(1), the lowest burning temperature (formerly /CTLIM/); OLDMOD_MOD (the previous model: HCOMPP; written by HPOINT and others); IOUNITS_MOD (the output unit).

The method

  1. Mixing. For a convection zone, the starting abundances are the mass-weighted means $\bar x_j = \sum_i x_{j,i}\,\Delta m_i / \sum_i \Delta m_i$ over the zone (elements 1–11), and so are the rates. If the zone's (or shell's) first point is below TCUT(1), it only homogenizes and returns.

    Question 1 (lines 196–222↗). Mass-averaged rates in convective cores. In a convection zone the rates themselves are mass-averaged and the zone burns as one, with the mean abundances. Averaging the rates rather than the products rate × abundances is exact only for instantaneous mixing. Is that adequate for ³He and the CN isotopes in convective cores, where the burning time of ¹²C can be shorter than the convective turnover time, and is a diffusive treatment of convective mixing planned? 2. Equations. Only the seven abundant species are followed, as the YREC paper's "full non-equilibrium burning" (§2.3.4, with ¹⁸O, step 4). The coefficients below show that a rate can stand for more than one reaction: $-3r_1X^2$ takes three protons per pp reaction (p+p→d and d+p→³He), and $-2X\,r_6\,{}^{14}\mathrm{N}$ two per ¹⁴N+p; ¹⁵N and ¹⁷O, "not currently used" (the comment), do not appear. Which chains each rate stands for, and why the nuclei between are skipped, is the question at the end of this step. Primordial deuterium and the light elements are burned separately, by DBURN and LIBURN. The rates HR$_j$ are, in the comment's words, "per gigayear per amu", to be "multiplied by the mass fractions of the reactants and the atomic weight (in amu) of the product to get the rate of change of the mass fraction", which gives the form below (how $N_A$ and the $1/A$ factors enter is question 4). With $\Delta t$ in Gyr and $A_k$ the species' mass numbers (1, 3, 4, 12, 13, 14, 16), the residuals are $$G_k(w) = w_k - w_k^0 - A_k\,\Delta t\,R_k(w),$$ where $w = (X, {}^3\mathrm{He}, Y, {}^{12}\mathrm{C}, {}^{13}\mathrm{C}, {}^{14}\mathrm{N}, {}^{16}\mathrm{O})$ are the end-of-step abundances (so the scheme is implicit) and, with $r_n$ the rate GRn and $f_3$ = HF1, $f_4 = 1-f_3$ (the branching of ¹⁵N + p to ¹²C + α or to ¹⁶O): $$R_X = -3r_1X^2 + 2r_2\,{}^3\mathrm{He}^2 - r_3\,{}^3\mathrm{He}\,Y - r_4X\,{}^{12}\mathrm{C} - r_5X\,{}^{13}\mathrm{C} - 2X\,(r_6\,{}^{14}\mathrm{N} + r_7\,{}^{16}\mathrm{O}),$$ $$R_{^3\mathrm{He}} = r_1X^2 - 2r_2\,{}^3\mathrm{He}^2 - r_3\,{}^3\mathrm{He}\,Y,$$ $$R_Y = r_2\,{}^3\mathrm{He}^2 + r_3\,{}^3\mathrm{He}\,Y + f_3r_6X\,{}^{14}\mathrm{N} + r_7X\,{}^{16}\mathrm{O} - r_{10}Y\,{}^{12}\mathrm{C} - r_8Y\,{}^{13}\mathrm{C} - r_{11}Y\,{}^{14}\mathrm{N} - 3r_{12}Y^3,$$ $$R_{^{12}\mathrm{C}} = r_{12}Y^3 - r_4X\,{}^{12}\mathrm{C} - r_{10}Y\,{}^{12}\mathrm{C} + f_3r_6X\,{}^{14}\mathrm{N},\qquad R_{^{13}\mathrm{C}} = r_4X\,{}^{12}\mathrm{C} - r_5X\,{}^{13}\mathrm{C} - r_8Y\,{}^{13}\mathrm{C},$$ $$R_{^{14}\mathrm{N}} = r_5X\,{}^{13}\mathrm{C} + r_7X\,{}^{16}\mathrm{O} - r_6X\,{}^{14}\mathrm{N} - r_{11}Y\,{}^{14}\mathrm{N},\qquad R_{^{16}\mathrm{O}} = f_4r_6X\,{}^{14}\mathrm{N} + r_{10}Y\,{}^{12}\mathrm{C} - r_7X\,{}^{16}\mathrm{O} + r_8Y\,{}^{13}\mathrm{C}.$$ The reactions by the comments: $r_1$ pp, $r_2$ ³He+³He, $r_3$ ³He+⁴He, $r_4$ ¹²C+p, $r_5$ ¹³C+p, $r_6$ ¹⁴N+p (with ¹⁵N+p), $r_7$ ¹⁶O+p (with ¹⁷O+p), $r_8$ ¹³C+α, $r_{10}$ ¹²C+α, $r_{11}$ ¹⁴N+α, $r_{12}$ triple α; $r_9$ (¹⁶O+α) and $r_{13}$ (¹²C+¹²C) are zeroed.

    Question 2 (lines 264–284↗). Which chains the rates stand for. The code follows seven species and its coefficients count three protons per pp reaction. That reads as if short-lived nuclei (¹³N, ¹⁵O, ¹⁷F, which β-decay within minutes) and those burned fast at these temperatures (²H made by the pp reaction, ⁷Be, ⁷Li, ¹⁵N, ¹⁷O) were taken to be processed at once, so that $r_1$ stands for p+p→d and d+p→³He, $r_3$ for ³He+⁴He through ⁷Be and ⁷Li to two ⁴He, $r_6$ for ¹⁴N+p through ¹⁵O and ¹⁵N+p, and $r_7$ for ¹⁶O+p through ¹⁷F and ¹⁷O+p, which would be why the CN cycle here has no ¹⁵N. Is that the design, and where does it stop holding (for ¹⁷O, say, at low temperature)? 3. Newton iteration. The Jacobian $\partial G_k/\partial w_l$ fills A(1..49) column by column and the residuals $G_k$ fill A(50..56) (the array B of rp_1, by EQUIVALENCE, now addressed as A(49+I)); entries below $10^{-8}$ off the diagonal are zeroed, and SIMEQC (which solves $J\,\delta = G$, leaving $\delta$ in A(50..56)) is skipped when the matrix is diagonal. Then $w \leftarrow w - \delta$; abundances below CMIN are set to 0. It stops when $\max|\delta_k| <$ ABSTOL and $\max|\delta_k/w_k| <$ RELTOL, or stops the run after KEMMAX iterations (STOP 1, the run-time error code). The first guess is the result for the shell below (or the shell's current HCOMP for the first shell).

    Question 3 (lines 349–360↗). The diagonal shortcut. When all off-diagonal entries are below $10^{-8}$, SIMEQC is skipped and the correction is the residual $G_k$ itself, not $G_k/J_{kk}$. The diagonal entries are $1 + A_k\Delta t\,(\dots)$, close to 1 only for small rates; is the undivided step intended?

    Question 4 (lines 234–237↗). A comment that no longer holds. The comment on the first guess speaks of calls from CRRECT with ITLVL > 1 ("USE THE PREVIOUS RESULT"), an argument now removed; the code uses the shell below, or the shell's own HCOMP for shell 1. Should the comment be updated?

    Question 5 (line 354↗; line 395↗). Hard-coded thresholds. $10^{-8}$ for zeroing matrix entries and $5\times10^{-7}$ for resetting $Z$. Are these still appropriate, or should they be settings beside CMIN, ABSTOL and RELTOL? 4. After convergence. ¹⁸O explicitly: $x_{18} = x_{18}^0 + 18\,\Delta t\,r_{11}\,Y\,{}^{14}\mathrm{N}$. If the previous model's $X$ at the zone's top (HCOMPP(1,IEND)) is below $5\times10^{-7}$, $Z$ is reset to $1 - X - {}^3\mathrm{He} - Y$ from the new values; otherwise $Z$ is the zone's mean at the start of the step. All shells of the zone get the same new values. Mixing is thus taken to be instantaneous: the YREC paper (§2.3.5) calls this usually an excellent approximation, which breaks down in such cases as AGB shell flashes.

    Question 6 (line 393↗). ¹⁸O is only produced. The explicit update adds ¹⁴N(α,γ) production but no destruction. Intended?

    Question 7 (lines 395–399↗). The switch for $Z$. Until the previous model's $X$ at the zone's top falls below $5\times10^{-7}$, $Z$ is held at its starting mean, although CNO processing and ¹²C(α,γ) change its parts; after, $Z = 1 - X - {}^3\mathrm{He} - Y$, so the C and O made by helium burning count as metals (as the opacity's $Z > 0.1$ branch in GETOPAC assumes). Why the switch at that threshold, and does $X + {}^3\mathrm{He} + Y + Z$ stay exactly 1 before it?

    Question 8 (lines 400–410↗; lines 121–127↗). ¹⁵N and ¹⁷O are not mixed in burning zones. When a convection zone burns, elements 8 and 10 of HCOMP are left unchanged (only the non-burning branch homogenizes all 11). They enter the Debye–Hückel composition ZDH (lines 141–142↗); do they matter anywhere else?

From YREC's documentation

Physics Options (Nuclear reaction & burning options) gives the solver's settings: CMIN (1.0D-20), the smallest abundance followed; ABSTOL and RELTOL (1.0D-5, 1.0D-4), the absolute and relative convergence criteria for the changes in abundance; KEMMAX (50), the largest number of substeps; and TCUT(1) (6.5), the $\log T$ above which $\epsilon_\mathrm{nuc}$ is computed, the other TCUT entries being thresholds for later burning stages. Its advice: only TCUT is adjusted from the defaults (6.5 is too high for deuterium burning on the pre-main sequence), and the others should not be changed without good reason.

Suspected bugs, by priority

(a) may give wrong results; (b) may crash; (c) misbehaves silently (a wrong message, an ignored setting); (d) fragile: correct today, but breaks on a change or an unusual input.

  1. (c) The error message gives a fixed limit of 50. (lines 377–385↗) When the iteration has not converged after KEMMAX iterations, the message says "RUN STOPPED AFTER 50 ATTEMPTS", while the limit is KEMMAX (50 by default, a namelist setting). With another KEMMAX, the message gives the wrong count. Read from the code, not run. A possible fix:
