Explanation

Purpose. MICRODIFF is the driver of YREC's newer treatment of gravitational settling (microscopic diffusion) of helium, metals and light elements. Its header (Garrett Somers, 05/2015) says it is adapted from GRSETT, "which is retained for backwards compatability", and "follows the formulism of Bahcall and Loeb 1989". It moves the model onto an equally spaced grid in radius, diffuses each species there, and maps the changes back onto the model's mass grid.

How it fits.

  • Inputs (arguments): the time step DT (s), the composition HCOMP(15,M), $d\ln P/dr$ (HQPR), $\log r$ (HR, cm), $\log\rho$ (HD), the unlogged mass HS1 (g), $\log T$ (HT), the convection flags LC, the number of points M and the total mass STOT. STOT is in grams; MICRODIFF_SETUP converts DT, STOT, HS1 and HQPR in place and MICRODIFF_ETM converts them back. The temperature gradient $\nabla = d\ln T/d\ln P$ (SDEL(2,·) in SCRTCH_MOD), named in the header, is read not here but by MICRODIFF_MTE.
  • Who calls it: MIX (when the settling flags are on and LNEWDIF selects the new routine) and ROTMIX (the same inside the rotational-mixing step).
  • What it calls: the four parts of the method, in order: MICRODIFF_SETUP (units and boundaries), MICRODIFF_MTE (model to equal grid), MICRODIFF_RUN (once per diffused species) and MICRODIFF_ETM (equal grid back to model).
  • Output: the updated HCOMP, in place.
  • Modules: NMLPAR_MOD, for the namelist switches LDIFY, LDIFZ and LDIFLI (read by PARMIN and shared with GRSETT, MIX, ROTMIX and the other MICRODIFF routines), and DIMENSIONS_MOD for JSON. The other members of rp_1's /GRAVST/, /GRAVS3/ and /GRAVS4/ (GRTOL, NITER_GS, FGRY, FGRZ, LTHOUL, LNEWDIF) are in NMLPAR_MOD too, but not used here.

The method. After the set-up (which returns at once for a fully convective model, or one with no hydrogen or helium left), up to three kinds of species are diffused, each by a call to MICRODIFF_RUN that returns the change in abundance on the equal grid:

  1. Hydrogen if LDIFY: column $j=1$ of the three-species vector ESPEC (X, Y, and a third species), with the metals as the third species, taken as iron ($A = 55.86$, $Z = 26$). Helium then follows from $Y = 1 - X - Z - X_4$ in MICRODIFF_ETM.
  2. Metals if LDIFZ: column $j=3$, again as iron.
  3. Light elements if LDIFLI: NLIGHT = 3 species in HCOMP rows CSLIGHT = 13, 14, 15, with atomic weights 6.015, 7.016, 9.012 and charges 3, 3, 4 (⁶Li, ⁷Li and ⁹Be: STARIN fills these rows from XLI6_INI, XLI7_INI and XBE9_INI), each in turn as the third species.

The changes EDELX, EDELZ and EDELLI are passed to MICRODIFF_ETM, which interpolates them back and applies them.

Grids. Variables prefixed E are on the equally spaced grid in radius; those also suffixed _H are at the midpoints between its points. Both are needed by the two-step Lax–Wendroff scheme.

KC's changes. 2025-05-30: CSLIGHT declared INTEGER (fixing -Wconversion); 2025-05-31: unused arguments dropped from the calls to MICRODIFF_MTE (HTU, LC) and MICRODIFF_RUN (NLIGHT, CSLIGHT).

Changed since rp_1. Declarations only: COMMON blocks to modules (the unused /CONST1/ and /LUOUT/ dropped), IMPLICIT NONE.

Questions for the experts

  1. Stale changes when a species is not diffused. MICRODIFF_ETM always applies EDELX (and EDELZ when LDIFZ). If LDIFY is false but LDIFZ or LDIFLI is true, EDELX is never set in this call; as a SAVEd local array it holds zeros only if the compiler zero-initializes static storage, or the values of an earlier call. Should EDELX be zeroed when LDIFY is false?
  2. The last light-element point. In the light-element loop, ESPEC(3,I) = ELI(II,NPT) relies on I equalling NPT after the preceding DO I=1,NPT-1 loop. It works in practice, but ESPEC(3,NPT) is clearer. Intended?
  3. Iron for the metals. All metals are diffused as one species with iron's weight and charge. Is this a deliberate choice (the heaviest abundant element) rather than a mean metal?
