Purpose

KCSURFP finds the gas pressure at the point where $T = T_\mathrm{eff}$ by interpolating in a table of model-atmosphere pressures as a function of $\log T_\mathrm{eff}$ and $\log g$. It is the version of SURFP for the newer Kurucz/Castelli tables (JNT 06/2014), whose size is $76\times11$ (NTC, NGC).

How it fits

  • Who calls it: only ENVINT, the envelope integration, which needs the pressure at the base of the atmosphere.
  • Inputs: TEFFL = $\log T_\mathrm{eff}$, GL = $\log g$, LPRT (print or not); the table in ATMOS2C_MOD (ATMPLC = $\log P$, grids ATMTLC, ATMGLC), filled by SETUPS, the module's only other user; in FAC_MOD, the per-temperature range of valid gravities IMIN2, IMINMAX2 (table entries that are missing in the grid).
  • Outputs: in ATMPRT_MOD, AP = $\log P$ and AT = $\log T_\mathrm{eff}$; ATMPRT_MOD is read by ENVINT, QATM, MASSLOSS and the other surface-pressure routines. It also sets JP and KP(4) in FAC_MOD (the table corner used). When LPRT is true it writes the result to the short output (ISHORT) and the model-storage unit (ISTOR), both from IOUNITS_MOD.
  • Calls: KSPLINE and KSPLINT (4-point natural splines), INTER3 (3-point Lagrange).
  • Modules: ATMPRT_MOD, ATMOS2C_MOD (with SETUPS), FAC_MOD (with SETUPS, SURFP), IOUNITS_MOD.

The method

  1. Stop if $\log T_\mathrm{eff} < 3.5$ or $\log g < -0.5$, with exit status 1.

    Question 1 (line 53↗; lines 79–80↗). Hard-coded table limits. The bounds 3.5 and −0.5 and the thresholds 4.5 and 4.55 in $\log T$ (above which only the highest gravities are used) are written into the code; they belong to one table. Are they right for every Kurucz/Castelli table the code may read? No upper bounds are checked, and SURFBC has no switch to gray for KTTAU = 5 (surfbc.f, question 1), so above the tables' last temperature (4.699 in input/atmos/CastelliKurucz/) the spline extrapolates silently.

  2. Take four table temperatures around $\log T_\mathrm{eff}$.

  3. At each of them, interpolate $\log P$ in $\log g$:

  4. for $\log T > 4.55$, linearly between the two highest gravities;

    Question 2 (lines 80–85↗). Linear extrapolation in gravity for hot stars. For $\log T > 4.55$ the linear formula extrapolates freely in $\log g$ beyond the two highest gravities (the tables in input/atmos/CastelliKurucz/ have no pressure below $\log g$ = 4.5 from $\log T$ = 4.602 up). Is that acceptable for the stars that reach these temperatures?

  5. for $4.5 < \log T \le 4.55$, by 3-point Lagrange (INTER3) over the three highest gravities;

  6. otherwise with a 4-point natural spline over the four valid gravities around $\log g$ (the top four if $\log g$ is above the second-highest one, the lowest four valid ones if it is below them).
  7. Interpolate the four results in $\log T_\mathrm{eff}$ with a 4-point natural spline: AP.

The comment (lines 46–49↗) is from an older routine: It calls the routine "SURFPL," says it interpolates in temperature and gravity with a 4-point Lagrange interpolator (the routine uses natural splines), and that "IT WILL QUIT IF THE DESIRED DATA POINT HAS TEFF OR LOG G MORE THAN ONE TABLE POINT FROM THE DATA" (only the lower limits are checked, line 53↗).

Question 3 (lines 46–49↗; lines 79–93↗). Comments that do not match. Not every row uses splines: For table rows with $\log T_\mathrm{eff} > 4.5$, gravity is interpolated linearly (above 4.55, lines 79–84↗) or with the 3-point Lagrange INTER3 (lines 86–93↗). Is that meant, and could a corrected comment describe both?

1. (never occurred) Missing pressures at the highest gravities.

(lines 97–111↗; lines 112–132↗) SETUPS sets IMINMAX2(J) below NGC when a temperature's highest-gravity entries are missing (pressure $\le 0$, e.g. −999) (setups.f, lines 327–358↗). The general search (loop over K from IMINMAX2(J)−3 down to IMIN2(J)) then covers $\log g$ only up to ATMGLC(IMINMAX2(J)-1). A $\log g$ between that and ATMGLC(NGC-1) matches no interval and falls through to the lowest four valid gravities, a long extrapolation; and a $\log g$ at or above ATMGLC(NGC-1) takes the top four gravities, missing entries included. Correct with the tables in input/atmos/CastelliKurucz/, which have all their highest gravities below $\log T$ = 4.5 (checked: every row of the 16 tables), but not with a table like the metal-poor Kurucz 1990 ones, where the same code in SURFP gives wrong pressures (surfp.f, suspected bug 1).
