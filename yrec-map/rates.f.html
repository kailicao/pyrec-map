Explanation

Purpose. Computes the nuclear reaction rates at one shell, for the abundance updates. Its header calls it "a stripped-down version of ENGEB": the same reactions, screening and constants as ENGEB, but without the energy generation. The rates are those of Bahcall's Neutrino Astrophysics (1989), with the alpha captures from Caughlan and Fowler (1988), as the comments say.

How it fits.

  • Who calls it: MIX (for each shell, before the composition is mixed and burned) and XTIME (at the shell IEND, for the time step).
  • Inputs: $\log_{10}\rho$ (DL), $\log_{10}T$ (TL), the mass fractions X, Y, XHE3, XC12, XC13, XN14, XO16, XO18, and the shell index IU.
  • Outputs: the arrays HR1…HR13 (the rates of the 13 reactions) and HF1, HF2 (two branching fractions) at element IU. KEMCOM reads them (all but HR9, HR13 and HF2) to update the abundances. MIX also copies all fifteen arrays into HCOMPM(1..15,·) of /BURN/, and ROTMIX reads them back from there.
  • Modules: CROSS_MOD (the derivative scalings QS0E, QQS0EE and the N15+p branching factors FO16, FC12, set in REMAP, shared with ENGEB); NMLPAR_MOD (the cross-section factors SSTANDARD(17), set in PARMIN or by REMAP; WEAKSCREENING, 0.03 by default in PARMIN; the temperature cuts TCUT); CONSTANTS_MOD (CLN $=\ln 10$, CC13 $=1/3$, CC23 $=2/3$).

The method.

  1. Composition sums. Over the 13 isotopes (n, H1, D, H3, He3, He4, C12, C13, N14, O16, O18, Ne20, Mg24; those of n, D, H3, Ne20, Mg24 set to zero) with mass fractions $X_i$, bare nuclear masses $A_i$ (ANUC) and charges $Z_i$: $$\mu_I^{-1} = \sum_i \frac{X_i}{A_i}\ (\texttt{AMU}),\quad \mu_e^{-1} = \sum_i \frac{Z_iX_i}{A_i}\ (\texttt{EMU}),\quad \texttt{ZET} = \sum_i\frac{Z_i^2X_i}{A_i},\quad \texttt{XTR} = \sum_i\frac{Z_i^{1.58}X_i}{A_i}.$$
  2. Below the temperature cut $\log T \le$ TCUT(1), all rates are zero.
  3. Electron degeneracy (for Salpeter's screening): with $\rho/\mu_e$ (RWE), $p_F^2/(m_ec)^2 = 1.017677\times10^{-4}(\rho/\mu_e)^{2/3}$ and $E_F/kT = 5.92986\,T_9^{-1}\bigl(\sqrt{1+p_F^2/m_e^2c^2}-1\bigr)$. The factor $f'/f$ (FPRF) is 1 for $E_F/kT \le 0.01$, 0 for $\log_{10}(E_F/kT) \ge 1.5$, and a sixth-order polynomial in $\log_{10}(E_F/kT)$ between.
  4. Screening. $\Lambda_0 = 5.9426\times10^{-6}\,T_9^{-3/2}\sqrt{\rho/\mu_I}$ (XXL), $\tilde\zeta = \sqrt{(\texttt{ZET} + f'/f\cdot\mu_e^{-1})\,\mu_I}$ (ZCURL), $\bar Z = \mu_I/\mu_e$ (ZBAR). For reaction $j$ with charge product $Z_1Z_2$ (ZPRD): $$U_\mathrm{wk} = Z_1Z_2\,\Lambda_0\,\tilde\zeta .$$ If $U_\mathrm{wk} \le$ WEAKSCREENING, weak screening is used, $U = U_\mathrm{wk}$. Otherwise the intermediate form $$U_\mathrm{int} = 0.38\,\Lambda_0^{0.86}\,\frac{\texttt{XTR}\;\zeta_{86,j}}{\mu_I^{-1}\,\tilde\zeta^{0.58}\,\bar Z^{0.28}}$$ (Graboske et al. 1973, with their coefficients Z86), or, for $U_\mathrm{wk} > 2$, the strong form $$U_\mathrm{str} = 0.624\,\bar Z^{1/3}\Lambda_0^{2/3}\Bigl[\zeta_{53,j} + 0.316\,\bar Z^{1/3}\zeta_{43,j} + \frac{0.737\,\zeta_{23,j}}{\bar Z\,\Lambda_0^{2/3}}\Bigr]$$ (USTR, with the coefficients Z53, Z43, Z23) when it is smaller than $U_\mathrm{int}$ or when $U_\mathrm{wk} \ge 5$. The rate is multiplied by $e^{U}$.
  5. Reactions 1–7 (pp, He3+He3, He3+He4, p+C12, p+C13, p+N14, p+O16), with $T_9 = T/10^9$ K: $$r_j = \rho\,\Bigl[T_9^{-2/3} + q_{1}T_9^{-1/3} + s'_j\,(q_{2} + q_{3}T_9^{1/3}) + s''_j\,(q_{4}T_9^{2/3} + q_{5}T_9)\Bigr]\exp\bigl(q_{6}T_9^{-1/3} + q_{7} + (q_{8}T_9)^2 + U_j\bigr)\;S_j,$$ with $q_{k} =$ Qk(j) (the terms of eq. 3.14 of Neutrino Astrophysics, as the comments explain), $s'_j$, $s''_j$ = QS0E(j), QQS0EE(j) (user scalings of the S-factor's first and second derivatives) and $S_j$ = SSTANDARD(j). Rates below $10^{-30}$ are set to zero. All are skipped when $X = 0$.
  6. Be7. Electron capture: $\lambda_e = 1.752\times10^{-10}\,T_9^{-1/2}\,[1 + 0.004(1000\,T_9 - 16)]\,\mu_e^{-1}\,S_{15}$. Proton capture: $\lambda_p = 3.128813\times10^5\,X\,S_{16}\,e^{-10.2625\,T_9^{-1/3}}$ times the bracket of step 5 with Q(8), and its own weak or intermediate screening. Both are multiplied by $\rho\cdot 8.584981\times10^{22}$, and $F_1 = \lambda_e/(\lambda_e+\lambda_p)$ is the fraction of Be7 that captures an electron.
  7. N15+p branching. The $(p,\alpha)$ and $(p,\gamma)$ channels have their own brackets, times $67500\,f_{C12}$ and $64\,f_{O16}$ (FC12, FO16); $F_3 = \mathrm{C12ALPHA}/(\mathrm{C12ALPHA}+\mathrm{O16GAMMA})$.
  8. Alpha captures (only when $\log T \ge$ TCUT(4)): C13$(\alpha,n)$O16 (`RATE(8)`), C12$(\alpha,\gamma)$O16 (`RATE(10)`), N14$(\alpha,\gamma)$F18 (RATE(11)) and the triple-alpha (RATE(12), $\propto\rho^2 T_9^{-3} e^{-4.4027/T_9}$), as fits of the Caughlan and Fowler form. RATE(9) and RATE(13) are always zero, and every rate at or below $10^{-5}$ is set to zero.
  9. Output units. Each rate (per second per gram, mass fractions left out) is multiplied by C21 $= 5.240358\times10^{-8}$, "(10^9 years/1 second)·(1 atomic mass unit/1 gram)", which gives HRj(IU). HF1(IU) $= F_3$ and HF2(IU) $= F_1$.

Design. All constants are DATA statements (double precision since rp_1), with earlier sets kept as comments (BP00, 7/21/03). The routine duplicates much of ENGEB, so a change to one must be copied to the other.

KC's changes. The arguments Z, XN15, XO17, XH2, XLI6, XLI7 and XBE9, which were not used, were removed from the signature (KC 2025-05-31), and the calls in MIX and XTIME changed to match.

Changed since rp_1. The constants are now double precision: the DATA values (masses, the coefficients Q1–Q7, the screening coefficients, C21) and the literals in the rate fits, which were single-precision constants; the exponent $2/3$ in $p_F$ and $\Lambda_0^{2/3}$ is now CC23 (it was 0.6666667 and 0.666667). The rates change by up to a few parts in a million, most where a constant sits in an exponent (Q7(2) = 76.6003 was off by $3.6\times10^{-6}$). The thresholds 1.E-2, 1.E-30 and 1.E-5 are still single precision. COMMON blocks to modules (/CTLIM/'s TCUT, SSTANDARD and WEAKSCREENING now in NMLPAR_MOD, /CTLIM/ and /WEAK/ dropped here), IMPLICIT NONE.

Questions for the experts

  1. The N15(p,α) S-factor. The comment says S0 "has been changed from 78000. to 675000. on 9/25/97", but the code multiplies C12ALPHA by 67500, ten times smaller. REMAP's reference value S0_1_15_C12ALP_REF is also 6.75D4, so the comment is probably the typo. Can this be confirmed?
  2. Two thresholds for zero. Reactions 1–7 are zeroed below $10^{-30}$, but at the end every rate at or below $10^{-5}$ (per second per gram, before C21) is set to zero. Is the second cut intended? At low density or temperature it could switch off slow but relevant rates.
  3. HF2 is never used. HF1 holds $F_3$ (N15 branching) and HF2 holds $F_1$ (Be7 branching). KEMCOM uses HF1 as F3. HF2 is only copied by MIX into HCOMPM(15,·) and copied back by ROTMIX; no routine computes with it. Is the Be7 branching meant to enter the abundance update (for example of Be7 or Li7), or can it be dropped?
  4. Comments that disagree with the code. "F3 IS THE FRACTION OF THE N14 THAT IS BURNED BY P, ALPHA" should read N15; the comment above the screening loop says WEAKSCREENING is passed "in the FLUX COMMON block", but it comes from NMLPAR_MOD (/WEAK/ at rp_1); "DL AND DT" are DL and TL.
  5. Hard-coded Be7 constants. The Be7 screening uses its own ZPRDBE7P = 4.0 and Z86BE7P = 5.7790, and no strong-screening branch. Fine for the Sun, but is this routine used for denser cores, where strong screening would matter?
  6. Duplication with ENGEB. Are the constants here guaranteed to match those in ENGEB? A check by script, or one shared routine, would remove the risk.
  7. Stale branching fractions below TCUT(1). When $\log T \le$ TCUT(1), the routine jumps straight to label 200, so F1 and F3 are not set in this call, and HF1(IU), HF2(IU) receive the values kept (by SAVE) from the previous call, for another shell. KEMCOM's loop in MIX stops at that cut for radiative shells, but is a stale HF1 ever used, for example in a convective zone whose first shell lies below the cut? Setting F1 = F3 = 0 before the jump would remove the doubt.
