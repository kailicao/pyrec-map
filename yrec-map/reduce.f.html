Purpose

One step of the elimination in the Henyey method: for the pair of shells $(i-1, i)$, it forms the residuals of the four difference equations of stellar structure and eliminates the unknowns of shell $i-1$, leaving relations for the corrections at shell $i$. Its name and the comments ("REDUCE MATRIX FOR PAIR OF POINTS (IM-1,IM)," in the caller) say this; that it is the forward sweep of a Henyey solver, with HSOLVE the backward one, is our reading of the algebra.

How it fits

  • Who calls it: COEFFT, once for each shell IM > 1, right after computing that shell's right-hand sides and their derivatives. For the first shell, COEFFT sets the central boundary conditions in the same arrays instead.
  • Inputs: the shell index I; the model's variables HP, HT, HR, HL (pressure, temperature, radius and luminosity, in the code's variables) and the mass variable HS; and for each equation $y \in \{P, T, R, L\}$ its right-hand side $f_y$ at shells $i-1$ (QP0, QT0, QR0, QL0) and $i$ (QP, …), with its partial derivatives (QPR is $\partial f_P/\partial R$, QTL is $\partial f_T/\partial L$, and so on; a 0 marks shell $i-1$).
  • Outputs: the arrays HHA(4,2,JSON) and HHB(4,JSON) at shell I (the reduced coefficients and right-hand sides), and HMAX(4), the largest residual of each equation so far. COEFFT returns them to CRRECT, which passes them to HSOLVE, the back-substitution that gives the corrections.
  • Modules: CONSTANTS_MOD, for CLN $= \ln 10$; DIMENSIONS_MOD, for JSON.

The method

Each structure equation is differenced with the trapezoidal rule. With $\Delta s = s_i - s_{i-1}$ (HS), the residual of equation $y$ is

$$b_y = \frac{\Delta s}{2}\,\bigl(f_{y,i-1} + f_{y,i}\bigr) - \bigl(y_i - y_{i-1}\bigr),$$

stored in HHB(k,I), with $|b_y|$ tracked in HMAX(k). The linearized (Newton) equations for the corrections $\delta y$ are, for each equation $y$,

$$\sum_{k}\Bigl[\Bigl(-\epsilon_{yk} - \tfrac{\Delta s}{2}\ln 10\,\frac{\partial f_{y,i-1}}{\partial y_k}\Bigr)\delta y_{k,i-1} + \Bigl(\epsilon_{yk} - \tfrac{\Delta s}{2}\ln 10\,\frac{\partial f_{y,i}}{\partial y_k}\Bigr)\delta y_{k,i}\Bigr] = b_y ,$$

with $k$ over $P, T, R, L$ ($\epsilon_{yk}$ is 1 when $k = y$, else 0; the factor CLN $= \ln 10$ because the corrections are in $\log_{10}$, as HP, HT, HR are base-10 logarithms (CRRECT adds them to these), while COEFFT's derivatives are with respect to natural logarithms, so $\partial f/\partial\log_{10}y = \ln 10\,\partial f/\partial\ln y$; for the linear $L$ column COEFFT divides by $\ln 10$ beforehand, as in its $\mathtt{QT}/(L\ln 10)$). Read from the algebra, the comments' columns are: 1–2 $\delta R_{i-1}$, $\delta L_{i-1}$; 3–4 $\delta P_{i-1}$, $\delta T_{i-1}$; 5–6 $\delta R_i$, $\delta L_i$; and $\delta P_i$, $\delta T_i$ remain. The work matrix Q(k,1:2) holds the coefficients of $\delta P_{i-1}$, $\delta T_{i-1}$, and HHA(k,1:2,I) those of $\delta P_i$, $\delta T_i$.

Question 1 (lines 42–111↗). The meaning of the columns. The comments name the pivots by row and column of a matrix that is written nowhere; the column map above is read from the algebra. Can it be confirmed, and documented in the code?

  1. Columns 1 and 2. The previous call left $\delta R_{i-1} = $ HHB(3,I-1) $- \sum_m$ HHA(3,m,I-1) $u_m$, and the same for $\delta L_{i-1}$ with row 4, where $u = (\delta P_{i-1}, \delta T_{i-1})$. Substituting them removes $\delta R_{i-1}$, $\delta L_{i-1}$. In the $L$ row the coefficient of $\delta L_{i-1}$ is $-1$ (no $\partial f_L/\partial L$ term), so its terms enter with a plus sign.
  2. Columns 6, 5, 4, 3, in turn ("PIVOT ON ROW-4 AND COLUMN-6," then row 3 and column 5, row 2 and column 4, row 1 and column 3): Gauss–Jordan steps that eliminate $\delta L_i$, $\delta R_i$, $\delta T_{i-1}$ and $\delta P_{i-1}$.

At the end, each of the four unknowns $x_k = (\delta P_{i-1}, \delta T_{i-1}, \delta R_i, \delta L_i)$ is given in terms of the two unknowns of shell $i$:

$$x_k = \texttt{HHB}(k,i) - \texttt{HHA}(k,1,i)\,\delta P_i - \texttt{HHA}(k,2,i)\,\delta T_i .$$

Rows 3 and 4 are what the next call (shell $i+1$) uses in step 1; HSOLVE fixes $\delta P$, $\delta T$ at the last shell from the surface conditions (HSRF), then applies this relation from the surface inward.

Design

No pivoting by size: the divisions by Q(2,2), Q(1,1) and $1 - \tfrac{\Delta s}{2}\ln 10\,\partial f_R/\partial R$ assume these are not zero. The routine has 39 arguments, the equations' derivatives one by one.

Question 2 (line 70↗; line 90↗; line 103↗). No safeguard against a zero pivot. DIV = 1.0D0/Q(2,2) and 1.0D0/Q(1,1) are taken without a check. Has a breakdown ever been seen, for instance in very thin shells?
