Explanation

Purpose. In the rotational-mixing and angular-momentum transport loop, a model time step is divided into shorter "diffusion" steps. MIDMOD builds the structure at an intermediate time of the model step by linear interpolation between the old model (start of the step) and the new one (end of the step). Its comments say it also accounts for nuclear burning, recomputes the angular velocity from the conserved specific angular momentum, and finds the edges of the central and surface convection zones and the moment of inertia of the surface convection zone.

How it fits.

  • Inputs: the new model in the arguments (HD, HL, HP, HR, HT, HG, the convection flags LC); the old model in OLDMOD_MOD, OLDPHY_MOD and OLDROT_MOD (HGO); the new auxiliary physics in SCRTCH_MOD (SDEL, SESUM, SEG, SO, SVEL) and TEMP_MOD (CP, HAMU, SQDT, THDIF, VISC); the fraction of the step FX and the diffusion step DT (of the model step DELTS); the specific angular momentum HJM.
  • Who calls it: only GETW, once per diffusion step, with FX = SUMDT/DELTS.
  • What it calls: OVROT (convective overshoot for the mixing zones), ROTMIX (burning and mixing over the short step), DBURNM (deuterium burning), and GETROT or SOLID (the rotation variables).
  • Outputs: the intermediate structure (HDM, HLM, HPM, HRM, HTM, HGM, LCM, LCZM), the rotation variables (OMEGAM, HIM, ETA2M, QIWM, R0M), IMIN, IMAX, HSTOP, HSBOT, HICZ, LCZSUR, LALLCZ, the mixing zones (MRZONE, MXZONE, NRZONE, NZONE), and the interpolated physics in MDPHY_MOD (DELM, DELAM, DELRM, VELM, VISCM, THDIFM, CPM, QDTM, OM, AMUM, EPSM, ESUMM), which GETW and the transport routines (CODIFF, MIXGRID, MIXCZ and others) read. It saves the composition after mixing in the argument HCOMPS (formerly /OLDAB/), an array GETW owns and passes on to SECULR.
  • Modules: NMLPAR_MOD (LSOLID, IMPJMOD, TCUT, LEXCOM), DEUTER_MOD (DRATE, DRATE0), CONSTANTS_MOD (CLN, CC23, CMSUN), OLDROT_MOD (the old model's HGO) and the modules above. The values of the previous intermediate step (DDELP, DDELN, HRP, LCP, formerly /PREVMID/) are local saved arrays.

The method.

  1. The first diffusion step (LFIRSTIT): HCOMP (rows 1–11, or 1–15 with LEXCOM) is reset to the old model's composition HCOMPP, and AMUM to AMUO, so that burning and mixing start from the start of the model step.

  2. Interpolation in time. For each variable $q$ (logarithmic structure variables and the physics), $$q_\mathrm{mid} = q_\mathrm{old} + f\,(q_\mathrm{new} - q_\mathrm{old}),\qquad f = \texttt{FX}.$$ The mean molecular weight AMUM is instead incremented: $\mu_\mathrm{mid} \mathrel{+}= (\Delta t/\Delta t_\mathrm{model})(\mu_\mathrm{new}-\mu_\mathrm{old})$. The total energy generation EPSM interpolates SESUM + SEG(6) + SEG(7). The interpolation is at fixed shell because the two models share a mass grid: when HPOINT rezones at the start of the step it interpolates the old model onto the new points (HPO, HTO, HRO, HLO, HDO in OLDMOD_MOD), and otherwise the main program copies the old model into them unchanged.

  3. Convection. A shell keeps its convective state if it is the same at both ends of the step; otherwise it is convective if $\nabla_\mathrm{ad} < \nabla_\mathrm{rad}$ at the intermediate time, with the larger of the two convective velocities.

  4. Receding convection zones (MHP 06/02): a block meant to correct the specific angular momentum of shells that leave a convection zone during the step. It is never run (question 1).
  5. Mixing and burning. OVROT sets the mixed zones with overshoot; ROTMIX burns and mixes over DT, using the intermediate temperature HTM "for thermodynamic consistency" (MHP 6/00). If the surface deuterium abundance at the start of the step (HCOMPP(12,M)) exceeds $10^{-14}$, the deuterium rate is interpolated and burnt by DBURNM, shell by shell in radiative zones (down to the burning cutoff TCUT(1)) and zone by zone in convective ones.
  6. Convection zone edges. IMIN $= \max(2, I-1)$, where $I$ is the first shell that is not convective, so it is the outermost shell of a central convection zone (the comment calls it "the first zone above" it; question 7), or 2; IMAX the lowest shell of the surface convection zone (or M). HSTOP is the total mass, HSBOT the mass at the base of the surface zone.
  7. Rotation. Unless rotation is forced to be solid (LSOLID, or IMPJMOD = 1), the first guess of each shell's moment of inertia per unit mass is that of a thin spherical shell, $i = \tfrac23 r^2$, so $\Omega = j/i$; GETROT then solves for the distorted shells. HICZ sums the moments of inertia of the surface convection zone. For solid rotation, SOLID gives the run, and HICZ sums all shells.

Design. A long block for a local convective turnover time (MHP 3/09) is commented out; GETW and other routines handle TAUCZ through OVRTRN_MOD. The routine prints to the screen (WRITE(*,*)) inside the dead block only.

Questions for the experts

  1. Dead code: the receding convection zone. LNEWCZ is set by loop 50 when any shell changes state, but then reset to .FALSE. at line 141, just before IF(LNEWCZ). The whole block that corrects $j$ for shells leaving a convection zone never runs. Was it switched off on purpose? If it is revived: (a) it overwrites the argument FX, which the caller uses; (b) RR = HJM2/HJM(J) is computed after HJM(J) = HJM2, so it is always 1; (c) the moment of inertia uses HR(II)-HRO(II) for the zone but HR(J)-HRP(J) for the shell; (d) in the envelope branch $J$ is summed over J+1..ICZ1 but rescaled over J+1..M.
  2. The mean molecular weight. AMUM is incremented by FAC2*(HAMU - AMUO) at every call rather than interpolated, so after the steps of one model step it reaches $\mu_\mathrm{old}+\sum(\Delta t/\Delta t_\mathrm{model})(\mu_\mathrm{new}-\mu_\mathrm{old})$, which is $\mu_\mathrm{new}$ only if the steps sum to the model step. Intended?
  3. Burning "accounted for here". The header says nuclear burning is accounted for here, but the update of HCOMP by HCOMPM (/BURN/) is commented out; burning now happens inside ROTMIX. The header could be updated.
  4. LCZSUR. The test (HSTOP-HSBOT)/CMSUN .GT. 0 is true for any surface convection zone; the comment speaks of one "deep enough for angular momentum loss". Was a threshold intended?
  5. Deuterium in radiative zones. GOTO 190 leaves both loops at the first shell below TCUT(1), so the remaining radiative zones (which may be hotter, deeper ones listed later) are skipped. Are the zones always ordered so that this is safe?
  6. HICZ for solid rotation. With solid rotation, HICZ is the moment of inertia of the whole star, not of the surface convection zone. Intended, since a solid body couples everything?
  7. IMIN. The comment says IMIN "is the first zone above a central convection zone", but IMIN = MAX(2,I-1) with I the first non-convective shell is the last convective one. Which is meant, given that IMIN is the first zone tested for rotational instabilities?
  8. The criterion for shells that change state. A shell whose convective state differs at the two ends of the step is called convective if $\nabla_\mathrm{ad} < \nabla_\mathrm{rad}$ at the intermediate time (DELAM(I).LT.DELRM(I)), the Schwarzschild criterion. Does this match the criterion the structure uses for that model (with any $\mu$-gradient or semiconvection treatment)? Near a receding convective core with a $\mu$ gradient the two can disagree, and a shell could be mixed here that the structure calls radiative.
