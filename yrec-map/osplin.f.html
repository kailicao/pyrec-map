Purpose

Interpolation by a shape-preserving osculatory quadratic spline, from ACM Algorithm 574 (D. F. McAllister and J. A. Roulier, ACM Transactions on Mathematical Software, September 1981), as the header says. It interpolates the table (XTAB, YTAB) of N points to the K abscissas XVAL, and returns the values in YVAL.

How it fits

  • Who calls it: the routines that move quantities between the model grid and an equally spaced grid: MIXCOM, MIXGRID, ROTGRID, GETGRID, EQ2MOD and HPOINT.
  • What it calls: SLOPES (the derivatives $m_i$ at the data points) and MEVAL (the evaluation; EPS $=10^{-4}$ is a relative error tolerance passed on to MEVAL's internal routine CHOOSE).

The method

The spline is made of quadratic pieces, with one or two knots added between data points where needed (CASES, CHOOSE), matches the values and the slopes $m_i$ at the data, and preserves the data's monotonicity and convexity wherever the data allow (hence "shape-preserving"); the details are in SLOPES and MEVAL. That is why it is used to move the diffusion coefficients, $\Omega$ and the abundances between grids: it does not overshoot, so regridding creates no new extrema, negative coefficients or negative abundances. The arrays are dimensioned JSON (from DIMENSIONS_MOD), the model's maximum number of shells.

Suspected bugs, by priority

(a) may give wrong results; (b) may crash; (c) misbehaves silently (a wrong message, an ignored setting); (d) fragile: correct today, but breaks on a change or an unusual input.

  1. (d) MEVAL's error code is ignored. (line 47↗; lines 93–98↗) MEVAL returns ERR = 2 when XVAL is not nondecreasing, and then returns without setting YVAL; ERR = 1 marks extrapolation, which is expected. OSPLIN does not check ERR, so with points of evaluation out of order the caller gets whatever YVAL held, without a message.
