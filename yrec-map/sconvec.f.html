Explanation

Purpose. Tests the edges of the first convection zone (every zone at rp_1) for semiconvection and, where the region next to it is unstable, extends the zone, following Castellani et al. (1971, Ap&SS 10, 340), as the header comment says. It then merges zones that have come to overlap.

How it fits.

  • Who calls it: MIX, before the composition is mixed in the convection zones, when semiconvection is on (LSEMIC) and from the second iteration (ITLVL > 1).
  • Inputs: the step DELTS (s), the model's $\log\rho$ (HD), $L$ (HL, in solar units, by the factor CLSUN), $\log P$, $\log r$, $\log m$, $\log T$ (HP, HR, HS, HT), the composition HCOMP, TEFFL, the mixing length CMIXL (passed on to TPGRAD), and the zones MXZONE(12,2) (bottom and top shell of each), NZONE.
  • What it calls: for each test point, EQSTAT (equation of state: $\nabla_\mathrm{ad}$, the mean molecular weights), GETOPAC (opacity) and TPGRAD (the radiative gradient $\nabla_\mathrm{rad}$).
  • Outputs: MXZONE and NZONE, changed; a line in the short output (ISHORT) for each edge it moves and each merger. Also, HD at the shells it tests is overwritten with the density returned by EQSTAT.
  • Modules: DEBHU_MOD (it loads XXDH, YYDH, ZZDH, ZDH(1..3) with each test point's composition for the Debye–Hückel correction in EQSTAT, when LDH is set), CONSTANTS_MOD, NMLPAR_MOD, IOUNITS_MOD (ISHORT), DIMENSIONS_MOD.

The method. For the first zone only (a local switch, L_ONLY_CORE, set to true; with it false, every zone) and each edge that is not the center or the surface (LFLAGD, LFLAGU); a fully convective star is skipped:

  1. At the first radiative shell outside the edge (JMR), compute $\nabla_\mathrm{rad}$ with the zone's composition. If $\nabla_\mathrm{rad} < \nabla_\mathrm{ad}$, the edge is stable. Otherwise keep $\nabla_\mathrm{rad,1}$, the mean molecular weight $\mu_\mathrm{CZ}$ (XINTMU = AMU + EMU), and $1 - \nabla_\mathrm{ad}/\nabla_\mathrm{rad}$.
  2. Recompute at the same shell with its own composition: $\nabla_\mathrm{rad,0}$, and the ratio $f = \nabla_\mathrm{rad,1}/\nabla_\mathrm{rad,0}$ (FDEL).
  3. The farthest the instability can reach in one step is the propagation speed (eq. 5′, p. 347 of the paper) times the step: $$R_\mathrm{max} = \Bigl(1 - \frac{\nabla_\mathrm{ad}}{\nabla_\mathrm{rad,1}}\Bigr)\,\frac{L}{10\cdot4\pi P\,r^2}\,\Delta t,$$ with $L$, $P$, $r$ at the zone's edge shell.
  4. Walk outward from the edge (down for the bottom edge, up for the top), shell by shell, accumulating $$R_\Sigma = \sum \Bigl(1 - \frac{\mu_\mathrm{CZ}}{\mu}\Bigr)\,\Delta r .$$ Stop when $R_\Sigma > R_\mathrm{max}$ (the limit, LMAX true) or when the shell is stable against the rescaled gradient, $f\,\nabla_\mathrm{rad} < \nabla_\mathrm{ad}$. The edge moves to the last unstable shell.
  5. Mergers. If the top of zone $j$ is now above the bottom of zone $j+1$, they become one zone, and the list is shifted down.

Changed since rp_1. Only the first zone in MXZONE is tested (L_ONLY_CORE, question 7); the mixing length CMIXL is a new argument, passed to TPGRAD; the unused /NWLAOL/ and /NEWOPAC/ are dropped; COMMON blocks to modules, IMPLICIT NONE.

Questions for the experts

  1. The radius is not updated in the walk. In the loop over shells II, every quantity passed to TPGRAD is reset for the shell, except RL, which stays at HR(JMR). TPGRAD's $\nabla_\mathrm{rad}$ does not use the radius, so the stability test is not affected; only the gravity $G m/r^2$ that TPGRAD computes for the convective case is, and its results are not used here. Still, should RL = HR(II) be added for safety?
  2. Overwriting the model's density. EQSTAT lists DL among its outputs (the density for the given $P$, $T$ and composition), and HD(JMR) = DL and HD(II) = DL write it back into the model. At JMR, the value kept is the one from the second call, with the shell's own composition; but in the walk it is computed for the shell as it is, before any mixing. Is this write-back needed, and is it safe for the next steps of MIX?
  3. The factor 10. $R_\mathrm{max}$ has $L/(10\cdot4\pi P r^2)$. Is the 10 from the paper's equation 5′, or a tuning factor?
  4. "Overshoot" in the comments. Comments speak of "OVERSHOOT" for what is semiconvective mixing here. A wording leftover?
  5. Touching zones. Zones are merged only when they overlap (MXZONE(J,2) .GT. MXZONE(J+1,1)), not when they become adjacent (top of one = bottom of the next − 1), which leaves no radiative shell between them. Should adjacency also merge?
  6. A merger keeps the upper zone's top. When zones $j$ and $j+1$ overlap, the merged zone takes the bottom of zone $j$ and the top of zone $j+1$ (MXZONE(J+1,1) = MXZONE(J,1), then the list is shifted). If the semiconvective extension carried the top of zone $j$ past the top of zone $j+1$, the merged zone would be smaller than zone $j$ alone. Should the top be MAX(MXZONE(J,2), MXZONE(J+1,2))?
  7. Only the first zone. A local switch, L_ONLY_CORE = .TRUE., set just before the loop over zones (no dated comment), limits the test to MXZONE(1,·), the innermost zone, whether it is a convective core or, in a star without one, an intermediate or the surface zone (whose bottom edge is then still tested). The name suggests that only a convective core was meant, and the header comment still says all convective regions are tested. Is this restriction meant to stay, and should it test MXZONE(1,1) = 1 (a core) rather than the first zone, or be a namelist parameter?
