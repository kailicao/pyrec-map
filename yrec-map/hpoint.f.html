Purpose

HPOINT rezones the model: at the start of each step it chooses a new set of mass points so that the jumps between neighbours in mass, luminosity, pressure, $X$ and $Z$ stay below user limits, keeps real discontinuities (convection-zone edges, composition jumps) as fixed points, and interpolates every model variable to the new points. It also adds or removes outer points when the outermost point drifts out of the allowed envelope temperature range, and recomputes the shell masses and, for rotating models, the rotation quantities.

How it fits

  • Who calls it: the main program, main.f, once per evolutionary step.
  • Inputs (arguments, changed in place): the model (HS = $\log m$, HP, HT, HR, HL, HD, HCOMP, LC, M), HSTOT ($\log M_*$), the envelope-fitting values PS, RS, ESTORE, ISTORE, the indices JCORE, JENV (edges of the convective core and envelope), JXBEG (H-shell base), LSHELL, the model number MODEL, the rotation arrays (OMEGA, ETA2, R0, HI, HJM, HKEROT, FP, FT, HG, QIW), the totals SJTOT, SKEROT, TEFFL, and CMIXL (the run's mixing-length parameter, which main.f sets from CMIXLA(NK); passed on to PHYSIC).
  • Outputs: the rezoned model; HS1 (mass at each point) and HS2 (mass of each shell); LRESET (true when outer points were added or removed); and in modules, the previous model on the new grid for the next step.
  • Calls: SPLINC and SPLINTD2 (splines to place the points), OSPLIN (an "oscillatory" spline for interpolating the variables); for rotation OVROT, MOMI, GETROT, FPFT; PHYSIC (the physics at the new points); SURFOPAC (a new surface opacity table when surface $X$ changed).
  • Modules, the main ones: NMLPAR_MOD (the namelist parameters: HPTTOL(1..12), the zoning limits, from PARMIN; TENV0, TENV1; LEXCOM, whether light elements 12–15 are followed; LROT; the overshoot settings LOVSTE, ALPHAE; the debug output LDEBUG, NPOINT); OLDMOD_MOD (after the rezoning, HSS, HPO, HTO, HRO, HLO, HDO and HCOMPP hold the start-of-step model on the new grid, and TEFFLO, HLSURO the start-of-step $T_\mathrm{eff}$ and surface luminosity, saved before the rezoning; read by KEMCOM, LIBURN, LIRATE88, CRRECT and others); ENTROP_MOD (the previous step's rates of change of $\log T$, $\log P$, $\ln L$, $\log r$, which main.f uses for its first guess of the new structure; shared with main.f only); DEUTER_MOD (DRATE0); OLDROT_MOD, OLDROT2_MOD, OLDPHY_MOD, DWMAX_MOD (rotation and the physics at the start of the step, for the angular-momentum transport in GETW and MIDMOD); SCRTCH_MOD, TEMP_MOD (the physics just computed by PHYSIC); COMP_MOD (XNEW, ZNEW, SENV); CONSTANTS_MOD; IOUNITS_MOD (ISHORT, IOWR, IDEBUG); JSON from DIMENSIONS_MOD; 14 modules in all.

The method

  1. Outer boundary. If $\log T$ at the outermost point is below TENV0, drop the outer points down to the first one hotter than TENV0 (and put the dropped mass into the envelope, SENV). If it is above TENV1, add one point from the stored envelope integration: $\Delta\log P$ from ESTORE, $\Delta\log T = 0.25\,\Delta\log P$, $\Delta\log r$ from ESTORE, the same $L$ and composition, and $\log\rho = \log P - \log T - 8$.

    Question 1 (lines 169–180↗; lines 806–813↗). The added outer point. $\Delta\log T = 0.25\,\Delta\log P$ and $\log\rho = \log P - \log T - 8$ are rough, hard-coded guesses, while ESTORE(2) holds $\log T$ at the stored envelope point. Are they only starting values for the next Henyey iteration, and would ESTORE(2) give a better one?

    Question 2 (lines 148–186↗; lines 184–185↗). TENV0, TENV1: stop or adjust? The documentation says the code stops when the envelope's temperature is outside $[$TENV0, TENV1$]$; HPOINT instead drops the outer points cooler than TENV0 (stopping only if the whole model is cooler) and adds one point from the stored envelope when the outermost point is hotter than TENV1. With the default TENV1 = 9.0, a point is added only if the outermost point is hotter than $10^9$ K. Is the documentation describing an older behavior?

  2. Fixed points. Flag the edge of the convective core, the base of the convective envelope, the base of the H shell, every point where $|\Delta X|$ > HPTTOL(3), $|\Delta Z|$ > HPTTOL(4), or (rotating models) $|\Delta\log\Omega|$ > HPTTOL(12), the base of envelope overshoot and the base of a finely zoned region around the envelope's base (HPTTOL(7) in $\log P$). At most 100 flags; sort them and remove duplicates.

    Question 3 (lines 190–228↗; lines 247–293↗). Flags beyond 100. The convection-zone edges and the H-shell base are flagged first, but when the composition and $\Omega$ tests reach 100 flags, the code jumps (GOTO 130) past the flags for the base of envelope overshoot and the base of the finely zoned region. Should those two be flagged before the gradient tests? (When the tests stop just short of 100, the two can run past the array: bug 2.)

  3. New mass points. Build a cumulative "distance" along the star, $$\chi_j = \chi_{j-1} + \frac{\Delta\log m}{\delta_m} + \frac{\Delta L}{\delta_L} - \frac{\Delta\log P}{\delta_P},$$ with $\delta_m$ = HPTTOL(2), $\delta_L$ = HPTTOL(9) times the largest $L$, and $\delta_P$ = HPTTOL(10) in the finely zoned region, HPTTOL(11) below it, HPTTOL(8) in the convective envelope (the $L$ term only where $L$ increases outward). Between consecutive flagged points, place points evenly in $\chi$, one per unit, and find their masses from a spline of $\log m$ against $\chi$. The idea: $\chi$ adds up the changes of $\log m$, $L$ and $\log P$ along the star, each in units of its tolerance, so that between neighbouring new points their sum is one tolerance; points crowd where any of the three changes fast. The code also sets PMAX1–PMAX5 (by their comment, the largest $\Delta\log P$ in each region), HPMAX(1), IIFLG, I0, and, for rotating models, $\log\Omega$ in OMEGAL, and declares ID(7) with its DATA; nothing reads them (unused?), and the placement uses HPTTOL directly (lines 229–239↗; lines 325–333↗; line 353↗; lines 359–361↗; line 128↗; line 137↗).

  4. Refinement in X and Z. Where $X$ increases outward between new points by more than HPTTOL(5), or $Z$ decreases outward by more than HPTTOL(6), insert evenly spaced points, stopping the run (STOP 1) if they would take the model past JSON points. Then drop points closer than HPTTOL(1) in $\log m$.

  5. Convection flags. Relocate JCORE and JENV on the new grid and reset LC.

  6. Interpolation. With OSPLIN, interpolate to the new points: the entropy-term variables (ENTROP), the composition HCOMP and the starting composition HCOMPP, the deuterium burning rate DRATE0, then $\log P$, $\log r$, $L$, $\log T$, $\log\rho$, and for rotating models $\Omega$, $j$, $f_P$, $f_T$, $r_0$, $\eta_2$; and the energy generation at the start of the step when needed (see bug 1).

    Question 4 (lines 648–665↗). Is rezoning conservative? Step 6 interpolates $X$, $Z$ and the isotopes with OSPLIN to the new mass points; spline interpolation does not conserve $\int X\,dm$, and over thousands of steps the error could accumulate (in the total helium, say, or in lithium). Is the total mass of each species checked after HPOINT, and how large is the drift over a main-sequence run?

  7. Masses. With $m_i = 10^{\log m_i}$: $$\texttt{HS1}_i = m_i,\qquad \texttt{HS2}_i = \tfrac12\,(m_{i+1} - m_{i-1}),\qquad \texttt{HS2}_1 = \tfrac12(m_2 + m_1),\qquad \texttt{HS2}_M = M_* - \tfrac12(m_{M-1}+m_M).$$

  8. Rotation. First guess of the moment of inertia $I_i = \tfrac23\,\Delta m_i\,r_i^2$; overshoot zones (OVROT); moment of inertia (MOMI); $\Omega$ from the conserved specific angular momentum (GETROT); the distortion terms (FPFT); MOMI again; then the totals $J = \sum j_i\Delta m_i$ and $K = \sum \tfrac12\Omega_i j_i \Delta m_i$, printed against the previous ones; and the old rotation state saved in OLDROT.

    Question 5 (lines 782–787↗). The JNT comment. JNT (2025-09-03) added the first call to MOMI, before GETROT, with the comment "WALPCZ does run in this version but I don't think it can hurt". Is the extra MOMI call still needed?

  9. Physics at the start of the step (rotating models, or light elements with envelope overshoot): PHYSIC at the new points; the results stored in OLDPHY, DWMAX, and the shear term $$\theta_i = \frac{\bar r^{\,4}}{G\,\bar m}\,\bar\Omega\,\frac{\Delta\Omega}{\Delta r}$$ (THO, with geometric means of $r$ and $m$ over the two points) and $d\Omega/dr$ (QWRST) in OLDROT2.

  10. A new surface opacity table (SURFOPAC) if surface $X$ changed by more than $10^{-8}$.

Design

It prints the flag points and the old and new numbers of points to the short output every step, and a full table of old and new points to the debug unit every NPOINT models when LDEBUG is on. Several module arrays of the old model (HPO, HTO, HDO) serve as scratch space before receiving their final contents.

From YREC's documentation

Physics Options (Rarely used options) gives TENV0 and TENV1 (defaults 3.0 and 9.0, in $\log T$) as the minimum and maximum temperature of the envelope integration, and says "Code stops below this" and "Code stops above this". The mesh tolerances HPTTOL that HPOINT applies are documented under their newer names on REMAP's page.

84    mass, L, P, X and Z below the user's limits (HPTTOL), keep
85    discontinuities as fixed points, add or drop outer points at the

87    points, recompute the shell masses and, for rotating models, the
88    rotation and the start-of-step physics.

95    rotation arrays (omega, eta2, r0, hi, hjm, hkerot, fp, ft, hg, qiw), the

123                                  f" OUTER POINTS DELETED OLD M ={m:5d}"
124                                  f"  NEW M ={i:5d}")

128                break  # to label 40
129        else:
