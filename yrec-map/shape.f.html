Purpose

Finds how much each shell of a rotating star is flattened. Its comment: "SHAPE FINDS THE DISTORTION OF A GIVEN SHELL FROM SPHERICAL SYMMETRY; IT CALCULATES THE CHARACTERISTIC RADIUS R0 AND ASSOCIATED PARAMETER ETA2. IN POLAR CO-ORDINATES THE RADIUS OF A SHELL IS R = R0(1 - AP2) … FOR MORE INFORMATION SEE ENDAL AND SOFIA."

How it fits

  • Who calls it: FPFT (the rotational correction factors $f_P$, $f_T$ of the structure equations) and SOLID (solid-body rotation).
  • Inputs: $\log\rho$, $\log r$, $\log m$ (HD, HR, HS), the angular velocities OMEGA, the range JSTART–JEND; from NMLPAR_MOD the tolerance ACFPFT and the iteration limits ITFP1, ITFP2; the constants from CONSTANTS_MOD. When JSTART > 1, the integration starts from the caller's ETA2(JSTART-1) and, in the second pass, R0(JSTART-1), so both arrays are inputs as well as outputs.
  • Outputs: ETA2 (Radau's $\eta_2$) and R0 (the characteristic radius) for each shell, which the callers use to compute the moments of inertia and the correction factors.

The method

With the mean density inside the shell $\bar\rho = m/(\tfrac43\pi r^3)$ and $r$ the radius of the sphere of equal volume (RPHI):

  1. Radau's equation (as the comment states it), $$r\,\frac{d\eta_2}{dr} + 6\,\frac{\rho}{\bar\rho}\,(\eta_2 + 1) + \eta_2(\eta_2 - 1) = 6,$$ integrated outward with a fourth-order Runge–Kutta step per shell, using the averages of $\rho$ and $\bar\rho$ at the midpoint (lines 91–118↗). At the first shell, $\eta_2 = 6(1 - \rho/\bar\rho)$ (line 60↗; a suspected bug, suspected bug 1). $\eta_2$ is 0 at the center (the comment's boundary condition, line 93↗), and it sets how strongly a shell is flattened, $A \propto 1/(2+\eta_2)$ (step 2). The code does not define $\eta_2$ beyond Radau's equation; what it stands for is question 1.

    Question 1 (lines 40–45↗; lines 92–93↗). What $\eta_2$ is. The code names $\eta_2$ only through Radau's equation. Is it the logarithmic derivative of the distortion amplitude, $\eta_2 = d\ln A/d\ln r_0$ (Radau's equation being Clairaut's equation for the shells' distortion rewritten in it, so that a shell's flattening depends on how the shells inside it are distorted)? FPFT's step 2 uses the same reading. If so, it should approach 3 at the surface of a centrally condensed star; does it in YREC's models? 2. The characteristic radius $r_0$ solves, by Newton's method (at most ITFP2 steps; the test compares the relative change of $r_0^3$ with ACFPFT itself, $10^{-36}$ by default, below double precision, so in practice the loop ends only when the update rounds to zero or after all ITFP2 steps: line 71↗, line 134↗, a suspected bug, suspected bug 2), $$r^3 = r_0^3\Bigl(1 + \tfrac35 A^2 - \tfrac{2}{35}A^3\Bigr),\qquad A = \frac{5\,\Omega^2 r_0^3}{3\,G m\,(2 + \eta_2)} .$$ 3. Iteration between the two (at most ITFP1 times, lines 122–162↗): the first integration assumes $r_0 = r$; if $r_0$ differs from that guess by more than ACFPFT$^{1/3}$, $\eta_2$ is integrated again in $r_0$ and $r_0$ recomputed.

    Question 2 (lines 137–140↗; lines 159–160↗). Tolerances compared with different quantities. In the outer loop, the first test compares the relative change of $r_0$ with ACC = ACFPFT$^{1/3}$, and the second compares the absolute change of $\eta_2$ (ERR = ETA2T - ETA2(I)) with the same ACC. Is that intended?

    Question 3 (lines 99–100↗; lines 144–156↗). Mixed variables in the second pass. In the second Runge–Kutta pass, the step is in $r_0$, but the midpoint densities RHOA, RHOBA are the same averages as in the first pass, taken at the volume radii. Is this approximation intended?

    Question 4 (lines 66–72↗; lines 124–162↗). Silent non-convergence. Neither the Newton loop (ITFP2 steps) nor the outer loop (ITFP1 passes) warns when it ends without meeting its tolerance; the last values are kept. Should it warn?

From YREC's documentation

Physics Options (Structural effects of rotation): ACFPFT (default 1.0D-36) is the fractional accuracy of the departures from spherical symmetry, given cubed (so $10^{-12}$ by default); ITFP1 (5) is the number of iterations between the equipotential shape and the effective radius, ITFP2 (20) that for the effective radius at a given shape parameter.

1. (harmless in the runs) The first shell's $\eta_2$ is seven times too large.

(line 60↗; line 93↗) The first shell takes $\eta_2 = 6(1-\rho/\bar\rho)$ (ETA2(1) = 6.D0*(1.0D0 - RHO/RHOBAR)). The series solution of Radau's equation as the comment states it (line 93↗) near the centre ($\rho = \rho_c(1-kr^2)$, so $\rho/\bar\rho \approx 1 - \tfrac25 kr^2$, and $\eta_2 = cr^2$) gives $7c = \tfrac{12}{5}k$, so $\eta_2 \approx \tfrac67(1-\rho/\bar\rho)$, a seventh of the code's value. What follows: The integration starts from a wrong $\eta_2$; the relative error decays outward as $r^{-7}$, so the effect on ETA2, R0, and so on FPFT's $f_P$, $f_T$ and MOMI's moments of inertia is probably small after a few shells. Only with rotation (LROT, off by default).
