Explanation

Purpose. A thin wrapper, "modified by G. Newsham from codes of N. Itoh & F. Timmes (9/06)": it computes the composition's mean nucleon number $\bar A$ and charge $\bar Z$, and passes them to SNEUT, which computes the neutrino losses. The header notes that only the carbon coefficients are used for bremsstrahlung losses ("the differences for other elements is slight"), and that the cutoff at $0.01\,T_\mathrm{Fermi}$ "is applicable for helium but will need to be amended for carbon and oxygen white dwarfs and cores of AGB stars".

How it fits.

  • Who calls it: NEUTRINO.
  • What it calls: AZBAR and SNEUT.
  • Outputs: SNU, DSNUDT, DSNUDD. SNEUT also returns the derivatives with respect to $\bar A$ and $\bar Z$ (DSNUDA, DSNUDZ), which are dropped here.

The method. With mass fractions $X_i$, nucleon numbers $A_i$ and charges $Z_i$, the molar abundances $Y_i = X_i/A_i$ give $$\bar A = \frac{1}{\sum_i Y_i},\qquad \bar Z = \bar A \sum_i Z_i\,Y_i .$$

Changed since rp_1. None: the file is the same as at rp_1.

Questions for the experts

  1. The Fermi-temperature cutoff. The header says the cutoff at $0.01\,T_\mathrm{Fermi}$ needs amending for C/O white dwarfs and AGB cores. Has this been done in SNEUT, or does it still limit the routine to helium cores?
