nulosses.f

97 lines · 2 definitions · on GitHub (7be4133)

Python version: nulosses.py, 65 lines, on the right (switch to Python above the code).

Purpose

A thin wrapper, "modified by G. Newsham from codes of N. Itoh & F. Timmes (9/06)": it computes the composition's mean nucleon number $\bar A$ and charge $\bar Z$, and passes them to SNEUT, which computes the neutrino losses. The header notes that only the carbon coefficients are used for bremsstrahlung losses ("the differences for other elements is slight"), and that the cutoff at $0.01\,T_\mathrm{Fermi}$ "is applicable for helium but will need to be amended for carbon and oxygen white dwarfs and cores of AGB stars". In SNEUT this cutoff chooses between the two forms of the bremsstrahlung losses, the weakly degenerate one above $0.01\,T_\mathrm{Fermi}$ and the liquid-metal one with the ¹²C parameters below it; it does not limit the routine, and the parameters are the same for every composition (settled from the code, Nov 9, 2026: lines 882–889↗).

Question 1 (lines 8–12↗; lines 882–889↗). The Fermi-temperature cutoff. The header says the cutoff at $0.01\,T_\mathrm{Fermi}$ suits helium but "will need to be amended" for C/O white dwarfs and AGB cores, and it has not been changed. Does it matter for the cores YREC follows (degenerate helium cores of red giants, and C/O cores if a run reaches them), and what cutoff would the C/O case need?

How it fits

  • Who calls it: NEUTRINO.
  • What it calls: AZBAR and SNEUT.
  • Outputs: SNU, DSNUDT, DSNUDD. SNEUT also returns the derivatives with respect to $\bar A$ and $\bar Z$ (DSNUDA, DSNUDZ), which are dropped here.

The method

With mass fractions $X_i$, nucleon numbers $A_i$ and charges $Z_i$, the molar abundances $Y_i = X_i/A_i$ give $$\bar A = \frac{1}{\sum_i Y_i},\qquad \bar Z = \bar A \sum_i Z_i\,Y_i .$$
