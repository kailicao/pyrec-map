Explanation

Purpose. Reads the OPAL 2001 equation-of-state tables (five hydrogen abundances) into memory. It is the 2001 version of READCO; the 2006 version is READCOEOS06.

How it fits.

  • Who calls it: ESAC01, on its first call.
  • Input: the table file, opened on unit IOPALE by PARMIN; closed here at the end.
  • Outputs, through COMMON: /AEOS/ (the tables XZ(5, 10, 191, 169), the grids and inverse spacings; shared with ESAC01, RHOOFP01, T6RINTEOS01, EQBOUND01), /BEOS/ (ZZ, read here; IRI, INDEX, NTA used), /EEEEOS/ (per-composition headers, only here), and /RMPOPEOS01/ (RMX, the table's highest density at each temperature, for the ramp in EQBOUND01, "mhp 7/2003").
  • Other COMMON: /OPALEOS/, /EEOS/, /EEEOS/ (XX), /AAEOS/, /LUOUT/.

The method. As in READCO (see there for the inverse spacings), with 169 densities and 191 temperatures, nine quantities per row instead of ten (the tenth slot keeps the marker $10^{35}$, and ESAC01 refuses iorder above 9, so it is never read), and ZZ read with more digits (F12.9). A zero temperature in the list stops the run with a message.

KC's changes. The members of /BEOS/, /EEEEOS/ and /OPALEOS/ were reordered (KC 2025-05-30); two DO loops that ended on an assignment (labels 12 and 13) were rewritten to end on CONTINUE (KC 2025-05-30).

Questions for the experts

  1. close (2). Before reading, the routine closes unit 2, which it never opened. No other routine opens unit 2 explicitly. A leftover from when the file was opened here?
  2. KRA hard-coded. The ramp's edge KRA is a DATA list of 191 indices, tied to this exact table. If the table were replaced, it would silently be wrong. Could it be computed from NTA or ICYCUSE?
