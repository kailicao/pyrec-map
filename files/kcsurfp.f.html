Explanation

Purpose. KCSURFP finds the gas pressure at the point where $T = T_\mathrm{eff}$ by interpolating in a table of model-atmosphere pressures as a function of $\log T_\mathrm{eff}$ and $\log g$. It is the version of SURFP for the newer Kurucz/Castelli tables (JNT 06/2014), whose size is $76\times11$ (NTC, NGC).

How it fits.

  • Who calls it: only ENVINT, the envelope integration, which needs the pressure at the base of the atmosphere.
  • Inputs: TEFFL = $\log T_\mathrm{eff}$, GL = $\log g$, LPRT (print or not); the table in COMMON ATMOS2C (ATMPLC = $\log P$, grids ATMTLC, ATMGLC), filled by SETUPS (with PARMIN and ENVINT sharing it); in FAC, the per-temperature range of valid gravities IMIN2, IMINMAX2 (table entries that are missing in the grid).
  • Outputs: in COMMON ATMPRT, AP = $\log P$ and AT = $\log T_\mathrm{eff}$; ATMPRT is read by ENVINT, QATM, MASSLOSS and the other surface-pressure routines. It also sets JP and KP(4) in FAC (the table corner used). When LPRT is true it writes the result to the short output (ISHORT) and the model-storage unit (ISTOR).
  • Calls: KSPLINE and KSPLINT (4-point natural splines), INTER3 (3-point Lagrange).
  • COMMON blocks: ATMPRT, ATMOS2C, FAC (with SETUPS, SURFP), LUOUT; it also declares ATMOS2 (the older table) without using it.

The method.

  1. Stop if $\log T_\mathrm{eff} < 3.5$ or $\log g < -0.5$.
  2. Take four table temperatures around $\log T_\mathrm{eff}$.
  3. At each of them, interpolate $\log P$ in $\log g$:
  4. for $\log T > 4.55$, linearly between the two highest gravities;
  5. for $4.5 < \log T \le 4.55$, by 3-point Lagrange (INTER3) over the three highest gravities;
  6. otherwise with a 4-point natural spline over the four valid gravities around $\log g$ (the top four if $\log g$ is above the second-highest one, the lowest four valid ones if it is below them).
  7. Interpolate the four results in $\log T_\mathrm{eff}$ with a 4-point natural spline: AP.

Questions for the experts

  1. Hard-coded table limits. The bounds 3.5 and −0.5 and the thresholds 4.5 and 4.55 in $\log T$ (above which only the highest gravities are used) are written into the code; they belong to one table. Are they right for every Kurucz/Castelli table the code may read? No upper bounds are checked.
  2. Comments that do not match. The comment calls the routine "SURFPL", says it uses 4-point Lagrange interpolation in temperature and gravity (it uses natural splines), and that "it will quit if the desired point has Teff or log g more than one table point from the data" (no such check exists beyond step 1).

  3. Linear extrapolation in gravity for hot stars. For $\log T > 4.55$ the linear formula extrapolates freely in $\log g$ beyond the two highest gravities.

  4. Missing pressures at the highest gravities. SETUPS sets IMINMAX2(J) below NGC when a temperature's highest-gravity entries are missing (pressure $\le 0$, e.g. −999). The general search (loop over K from IMINMAX2(J)−3 down to IMIN2(J)) then covers $\log g$ only up to ATMGLC(IMINMAX2(J)-1). A $\log g$ between that and ATMGLC(NGC-1) matches no interval and falls through to the lowest four valid gravities, a long extrapolation; and a $\log g$ at or above ATMGLC(NGC-1) takes the top four gravities, missing entries included. Should both branches use IMINMAX2(J) in place of NGC?
